Physics • Fluid Mechanics & Pressure CISCE Model Examination • [3 Marks]
A rectangular solid block of iron having mass \(12\text{ kg}\) has dimensions \(20\text{ cm} \times 10\text{ cm} \times 5\text{ cm}\). Calculate: (i) The maximum pressure, and (ii) The minimum pressure that the block can exert on a horizontal surface. [Take acceleration due to gravity \(g = 10\text{ m/s}^2\)]
Examiner Guidance: Pressure is inversely proportional to the contact surface area (\(P = F/A\)). The force (thrust) is constant and equal to the weight of the block (\(W = m \cdot g\)). Hence, maximum pressure occurs on the smallest surface face, while minimum pressure occurs on the largest surface face. Convert all dimensions to SI metres before multiplying.
Step-by-step Evaluator Marking Scheme: 1. Applied Thrust (Weight):
    $$F = m \cdot g = 12\text{ kg} \times 10\text{ m/s}^2 = \mathbf{120\text{ N}} \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$
2. Maximum Pressure (Smallest Area):
    \(A_{\text{min}} = 0.10\text{ m} \times 0.05\text{ m} = 0.005\text{ m}^2\)
    $$P_{\text{max}} = \frac{120\text{ N}}{0.005\text{ m}^2} = \mathbf{24,000\text{ Pa (or N/m}^2\mathbf{)}} \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$
3. Minimum Pressure (Largest Area):
    \(A_{\text{max}} = 0.20\text{ m} \times 0.10\text{ m} = 0.02\text{ m}^2\)
    $$P_{\text{min}} = \frac{120\text{ N}}{0.02\text{ m}^2} = \mathbf{6,000\text{ Pa (or N/m}^2\mathbf{)}} \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$
Physics • Work, Energy & Machines CISCE Model Examination • [3 Marks]
A crowbar \(2.0\text{ m}\) long is pivoted at a point \(40\text{ cm}\) from a load of \(750\text{ N}\). Calculate: (i) The Mechanical Advantage (MA) of the crowbar, and (ii) The minimum effort required to lift the load.
Examiner Guidance: A crowbar is a Class 1 lever with the fulcrum situated between the load and the effort. Mechanical Advantage is the ratio of Effort Arm to Load Arm (\(MA = \frac{\text{Effort Arm}}{\text{Load Arm}}\)). Remember that total crowbar length equals the sum of load arm and effort arm.
Step-by-step Evaluator Marking Scheme: 1. Effort Arm Length:
    \(\text{Total Length} = 2.0\text{ m} = 200\text{ cm}\)
    \(\text{Load Arm} = 40\text{ cm}\)
    \(\text{Effort Arm} = 200\text{ cm} - 40\text{ cm} = \mathbf{160\text{ cm}}\) [0.5 Mark]

2. Mechanical Advantage (MA):
    $$MA = \frac{\text{Effort Arm}}{\text{Load Arm}} = \frac{160\text{ cm}}{40\text{ cm}} = \mathbf{4} \quad \text{[1.5 Marks]}$$
3. Minimum Effort (\(E\)):
    $$E = \frac{\text{Load}}{MA} = \frac{750\text{ N}}{4} = \mathbf{187.5\text{ N}} \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$
Chemistry • Reactions & Equation Balancing CISCE Model Examination • [3 Marks]
Balance the following skeletal chemical equations and identify the type of chemical reaction in each case:

(a) \(\text{KClO}_3\text{ (s)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{KCl (s)} + \text{O}_2\text{ (g)}\)
(b) \(\text{Fe (s)} + \text{CuSO}_4\text{ (aq)} \longrightarrow \text{FeSO}_4\text{ (aq)} + \text{Cu (s)}\)
Examiner Guidance: Apply the Law of Conservation of Mass. For reaction (a), look at oxygen: 3 atoms on the left and 2 on the right; their lowest common multiple is 6. For reaction (b), verify if single element displacement is taking place according to the Activity Series.
Step-by-step Evaluator Marking Scheme: 1. Part (a) Balanced Equation:
    $$\mathbf{2KClO_3\text{ (s)} \xrightarrow[\text{MnO}_2]{\Delta} 2KCl\text{ (s)} + 3O_2\uparrow\text{ (g)}}$$
    • Reaction Type: Thermal Decomposition Reaction. [1.5 Marks]

2. Part (b) Balanced Equation:
    $$\mathbf{Fe\text{ (s)} + CuSO_4\text{ (aq, blue)} \longrightarrow FeSO_4\text{ (aq, green)} + Cu\text{ (s, reddish ppt)}}$$
    • Reaction Type: Single Displacement (Redox) Reaction. [1.5 Marks]
Biology • Human Circulatory System CISCE Model Examination • [3 Marks]
Give precise scientific reasons for the following anatomical features of the human cardiovascular system:

(a) The left ventricle of the heart has a much thicker muscular wall than the right ventricle.
(b) Veins contain internal pocket-shaped valves, whereas arteries do not.
Examiner Guidance: Focus on pressure dynamics and destination distances. The left ventricle pumps blood throughout the entire body (systemic circulation), while the right ventricle pumps only to the adjacent lungs (pulmonary circulation). For veins, consider the low-pressure steady flow against gravity.
Step-by-step Evaluator Marking Scheme: 1. Part (a) Scientific Reason:
    The left ventricle must generate powerful hydrostatic pressure to pump oxygenated blood to all distant tissues of the body through the aorta (systemic circulation). In contrast, the right ventricle pumps blood only a short distance to the neighboring lungs (pulmonary circulation). [1.5 Marks]

2. Part (b) Scientific Reason:
    Blood in veins flows under very low pressure in a steady stream, often moving upwards against gravity; semilunar pocket valves prevent the backward flow of blood. In arteries, blood surges rapidly under high pulsating pressure directly from the heart, preventing any backflow. [1.5 Marks]
