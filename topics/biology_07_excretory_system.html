Biology • Topic 07

Human Excretory System & Kidney Nephrons

The removal of toxic metabolic nitrogenous wastes (urea, uric acid), gross renal anatomy, microscopic structural nephrons, and the three stages of urine filtration and osmoregulation.

1. Excretion vs. Egestion

Excretion: The biological process of separating and eliminating toxic, nitrogenous metabolic end-products (chiefly urea, creatinine, uric acid, and excess mineral salts) generated by cellular metabolism from the bloodstream.

• Crucial Examiner Distinction: Egestion is the discharge of undigested, unabsorbed food waste from the alimentary canal through the anus (which has never participated in cellular metabolism). Excretion deals exclusively with metabolic chemical wastes filtered from the blood by the kidneys.

2. Gross Anatomy of the Human Urinary System

1. Pair of Kidneys: Bean-shaped, reddish-brown organs located on the posterior abdominal wall on either side of the vertebral column. The outer region is the renal cortex; the inner region is the renal medulla.
2. Pair of Ureters: Slender muscular tubes emerging from the renal pelvis that propel urine downward into the bladder via peristaltic waves.
3. Urinary Bladder: Elastic muscular pear-shaped reservoir that temporarily stores urine (up to \(\approx 500\text{--}800\text{ mL}\)).
4. Urethra: Canal guarded by a muscular sphincter that expels urine outside the body during micturition.

3. The Nephron: Three Stages of Urine Formation

1. Ultrafiltration (in Malpighian Corpuscle): Blood enters the glomerulus (tuft of capillaries) under extremely high hydrostatic pressure via the wide afferent arteriole. Water, glucose, amino acids, urea, and mineral salts are forced across the porous glomerular membrane into Bowman's Capsule to form the glomerular filtrate. (Blood cells and large plasma proteins are retained).
2. Selective Reabsorption (in Renal Tubules): As the filtrate passes through the Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT) and Loop of Henle, 100% of essential glucose and amino acids, and \(\approx 99\%\) of water and essential sodium/chloride ions are reabsorbed back into the surrounding peritubular capillary blood.
3. Tubular Secretion: Excess potassium ions, hydrogen ions, creatinine, and drug metabolites are actively secreted from peritubular capillaries into the Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT) to form concentrated, hypertonic urine.
