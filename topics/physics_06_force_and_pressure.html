Physics • Topic 06

Force, Thrust & Hydrostatic Fluid Pressure

The mechanics of perpendicular force (thrust), pressure distribution (\(P = F/A\)), the mathematical derivation of liquid pressure with depth (\(P = h\rho g\)), Pascal's law of fluid transmission, and atmospheric pressure.

1. Thrust vs. Pressure

When a solid rests on a surface, it exerts a downward force equal to its total weight. The physical effect of this force depends entirely on the contact area over which it is distributed.

Thrust (\(F_\perp\)): The total perpendicular force exerted by a body on a contact surface. It is a vector quantity measured in Newtons (N).
Pressure (\(P\)): The thrust exerted per unit area of contact. It is a scalar quantity. $$P = \frac{\text{Thrust } (F)}{\text{Surface Area } (A)} \quad \left[\text{SI Unit: Pascal (Pa)} = 1\text{ N/m}^2\right]$$ Consequence: As contact area \(A \to 0\), pressure \(P \to \infty\). This explains why a sharp needle penetrates cloth effortlessly while a blunt rod fails.

2. Derivation of Hydrostatic Pressure at Depth

Mathematical Proof: \(P = h \cdot \rho \cdot g\)
1. Consider an imaginary cylindrical liquid column of uniform cross-sectional area \(A\), submerged to depth \(h\) in a liquid of density \(\rho\).
2. The total volume of liquid contained in the cylinder is: $$V = \text{Area} \times \text{Height} = A \cdot h$$
3. The mass of this liquid column is: $$m = \text{Volume} \times \text{Density} = A \cdot h \cdot \rho$$
4. The downward thrust (weight) acting on base area \(A\) is: $$F = m \cdot g = A \cdot h \cdot \rho \cdot g$$
5. Dividing thrust by base area \(A\) gives the hydrostatic pressure: $$P = \frac{F}{A} = \frac{A \cdot h \cdot \rho \cdot g}{A} = \mathbf{h \cdot \rho \cdot g}$$
6. Conclusion: Liquid pressure is directly proportional to depth (\(h\)), liquid density (\(\rho\)), and local acceleration due to gravity (\(g\)), and is completely independent of the shape of the container.

3. Pascal's Law of Liquid Transmission

Pascal's Principle: Pressure applied to an enclosed, incompressible liquid is transmitted equally and undiminished in all directions throughout the liquid and to the walls of the containing vessel.

• Hydraulic Press / Lift Equation: $$\frac{F_1}{A_1} = \frac{F_2}{A_2} \implies F_2 = F_1 \times \left(\frac{A_2}{A_1}\right)$$ Since \(A_2 \gg A_1\), a small effort force \(F_1\) can lift a massive vehicle weight \(F_2\).

4. Formal Exemplar CISCE Numerical

Exemplar Problem: Hydrostatic Pressure [3 Marks]
Calculate the hydrostatic pressure exerted by water at the bottom of a swimming pool of depth 3.5 meters. [Take density of water \(\rho = 1000\text{ kg/m}^3\) and \(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)]
Evaluator Solution Breakdown:

1. Formula Declaration:
    \(P = h \cdot \rho \cdot g\) [1 Mark]

2. Substitution of Standard Values:
    \(P = 3.5\text{ m} \times 1,000\text{ kg/m}^3 \times 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\) [1 Mark]

3. Final Computed Result:
    \(\mathbf{P = 34,300\text{ Pa (or } 3.43 \times 10^4\text{ N/m}^2\mathbf{)}}\) [1 Mark]
