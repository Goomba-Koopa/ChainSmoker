Biology Chapter 7 Batch Topic #22

Human Excretory System, Kidney Anatomy & Nephron Physiology

Textbook exposition: Primary nitrogenous wastes (Urea, Uric Acid, Ammonia), Urinary tract anatomy (Kidneys, Ureters, Urinary Bladder, Urethra), Nephron structure (Bowman's Capsule, Glomerulus, Henle's Loop), and the Two-step Urine Formation physiology (Ultrafiltration & Selective Reabsorption).

1.0 Organs of the Human Urinary System

1. Kidneys (Pair of Bean-shaped Organs)

Located on either side of the vertebral column. Outer Cortex (dark red) and inner Medulla (renal pyramids).

2. Ureters & Urinary Bladder

Two muscular tubes conveying urine by peristalsis to the distensible reservoir (Urinary Bladder), voided via Urethra.

2.0 Physiology of Urine Formation in the Nephron

Step 1: Ultrafiltration (In Glomerulus)

Afferent arteriole is wider than efferent arteriole, creating tremendous hydrostatic pressure in the glomerulus. Water, urea, glucose, salts filter into Bowman's capsule as glomerular filtrate (blood cells and large proteins remain behind).

Step 2: Selective Reabsorption (In Renal Tubule)

As filtrate flows through proximal tubule and loop of Henle, 100% of vital glucose, amino acids, and major water are actively reabsorbed into peritubular capillaries, leaving concentrated hypertonic Urine (95% Water + 2.5% Urea + 2.5% Salts).
