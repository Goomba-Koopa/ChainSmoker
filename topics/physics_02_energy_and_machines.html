Physics • Topic 02

Energy, Mechanical Work & Simple Machines

The scientific definition of work (\(W = F \times s\)), mechanical energy forms (Kinetic vs Potential), the Principle of Conservation of Energy, and lever mechanics (Class 1, 2, and 3).

1. Scientific Concept of Work & Units

Definition of Work (\(W\)): Work is said to be done when an applied force (\(F\)) acts on a body and produces a non-zero displacement (\(s\)) in the body along the direction of the force: $$W = F \times s$$
• Two Mandatory Conditions for Work:
1. A force must act on the body (\(F \neq 0\)).
2. The body must actually be displaced in the direction of the force (\(s \neq 0\)).

• Crucial Examiner Insight: A coolie holding a heavy trunk of \(50\text{ kg}\) on their head while standing stationary on a platform does zero work (\(W = 0\)) in physics, because displacement \(s = 0\), despite physical fatigue.
SI vs. CGS Units & Dimensional Equivalence
• SI Unit: \(\text{Joule (J)} = 1\text{ Newton (N)} \times 1\text{ metre (m)}\)
• CGS Unit: \(\text{erg} = 1\text{ dyne} \times 1\text{ cm}\)

• Derivation of Joule to Erg Equivalence:
$$1\text{ Joule} = 1\text{ N} \times 1\text{ m} = (10^5\text{ dynes}) \times (10^2\text{ cm}) = \mathbf{10^7\text{ ergs}}$$

2. Kinetic vs. Potential Mechanical Energy

Characteristic Kinetic Energy (\(E_k\) or \(K\)) Potential Energy (\(E_p\) or \(U\))
Origin Energy possessed by a body by virtue of its state of motion. Energy possessed by a body by virtue of its position or deformed shape.
Mathematical Formula $$K = \frac{1}{2} m v^2$$ $$U = m g h \quad \text{(Gravitational PE)}$$
Dependence Directly proportional to mass \(m\) and the square of velocity \(v^2\). Directly proportional to mass \(m\), acceleration due to gravity \(g\), and height \(h\).
Velocity Dependency Becomes zero when the body comes to rest (\(v = 0\)). Does not depend on the instantaneous velocity of the body.
Canonical Examples Speeding bullet, flowing river, rotating turbine wheel. Water stored behind a high dam, wound watch spring, stretched bow.

3. Lever Mechanics: Fulcrum, Load & Effort

Fundamental Ratios of Simple Machines
• Mechanical Advantage (MA): \(\displaystyle MA = \frac{\text{Load } (L)}{\text{Effort } (E)}\)
• Velocity Ratio (VR): \(\displaystyle VR = \frac{\text{Displacement of Effort } (d_E)}{\text{Displacement of Load } (d_L)}\)
• Efficiency (\(\eta\)): \(\displaystyle \eta = \frac{\text{Work Output}}{\text{Work Input}} = \frac{MA}{VR} \times 100\%\)
• For an ideal frictionless machine: \(MA = VR\) (\(\eta = 100\%\)). In real machines, friction makes \(MA < VR\) (\(\eta < 100\%\)).
Class of Lever Central Position Mechanical Advantage (MA) Primary ICSE Examples
Class I Lever Fulcrum (F) lies between Load and Effort (\(L\text{--}F\text{--}E\)) Can be \(> 1\), \(= 1\), or \(< 1\) Crowbar, pair of scissors, seesaw, pliers, beam balance
Class II Lever Load (L) lies between Fulcrum and Effort (\(F\text{--}L\text{--}E\)) Always \(MA > 1\) (Acts as a force multiplier) Nutcracker, wheelbarrow, bottle opener, paper cutter
Class III Lever Effort (E) lies between Fulcrum and Load (\(F\text{--}E\text{--}L\)) Always \(MA < 1\) (Acts as a speed/distance multiplier) Pair of sugar tongs, human forearm lifting weight, fishing rod

4. Worked CISCE Exemplar Numericals

Exemplar 2.1: Lever Principle of Moments [3 Marks]
A crowbar of total length \(150\text{ cm}\) is used to lift a rock of weight \(600\text{ N}\). The fulcrum is placed at a distance of \(30\text{ cm}\) from the rock. Calculate: (i) The Effort arm, (ii) The Mechanical Advantage (MA), and (iii) The minimum Effort required.
Step-by-Step Examiner Solution:

1. Load Arm & Effort Arm:
    Load Arm = \(30\text{ cm}\)
    Effort Arm = \(\text{Total Length} - \text{Load Arm} = 150 - 30 = \mathbf{120\text{ cm}}\) [1 Mark]

2. Mechanical Advantage:
    $$MA = \frac{\text{Effort Arm}}{\text{Load Arm}} = \frac{120\text{ cm}}{30\text{ cm}} = \mathbf{4}$$ [1 Mark]

3. Minimum Effort Required:
    $$E = \frac{\text{Load}}{MA} = \frac{600\text{ N}}{4} = \mathbf{150\text{ N}}$$ [1 Mark]
