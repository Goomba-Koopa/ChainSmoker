Chemistry • Topic 06

Acids, Bases, Salts & Chemical Indicators

The chemistry of proton donors and acceptors, physical and chemical properties of acids and alkalis, neutralization reaction dynamics, and the master indicator color transition ledger.

1. Acids, Bases & Alkalis

Acid: A chemical compound which, when dissolved in aqueous solution, ionizes to release hydrogen ions (\(\text{H}^+\) or hydronium \(\text{H}_3\text{O}^+\)) as the only positive ions.
• Mineral (Strong) Acids: \(\text{HCl}\) (Hydrochloric), \(\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4\) (Sulfuric), \(\text{HNO}_3\) (Nitric).
• Organic (Weak) Acids: \(\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}\) (Acetic/Vinegar), \(\text{C}_6\text{H}_8\text{O}_7\) (Citric in lemons).
Base & Alkali: A base is a metallic oxide or metallic hydroxide that reacts with an acid to produce salt and water only. An alkali is a water-soluble base that releases hydroxyl ions (\(\text{OH}^-\)) in aqueous solution (e.g., \(\text{NaOH}, \text{KOH}, \text{Ca(OH)}_2\)).
Axiom: "All alkalis are bases, but not all bases are alkalis" (e.g., \(\text{Cu(OH)}_2\) is an insoluble base, hence not an alkali).

3. Neutralization Reactions & Salt Formation

Neutralization: The exothermic chemical reaction between an acid and a base to form a neutral salt and water: $$\mathbf{\text{Acid} + \text{Base} \longrightarrow \text{Salt} + \text{Water} + \text{Heat}}$$ $$\text{HCl (aq)} + \text{NaOH (aq)} \longrightarrow \text{NaCl (aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{O (l)}$$ $$\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4\text{ (aq)} + 2\text{KOH (aq)} \longrightarrow \text{K}_2\text{SO}_4\text{ (aq)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O (l)}$$
