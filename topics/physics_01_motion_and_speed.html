Physics • Topic 01

Motion, Distance, Displacement & Speed

The relative nature of rest and motion, translatory vs rotatory systems, scalar distance vs vector displacement, uniform vs non-uniform speed, and algebraic conversion proofs.

1. The Relativity of Rest and Motion

Rest & Motion Postulate: A body is said to be at rest if it does not change its position with respect to a fixed reference point (surroundings) with time. A body is in motion if its position continuously changes with respect to its surroundings.

• Crucial Examiner Insight: Rest and motion are purely relative terms. Absolute rest or absolute motion does not exist in the physical universe. A passenger seated inside a moving railway carriage is at rest relative to co-passengers, yet in rapid motion relative to trees, telephone poles, and platforms outside.

2. Distance vs. Displacement

Characteristic Distance (\(s\)) Displacement (\(\vec{s}\))
Definition Total length of the actual path traversed by a moving body irrespective of direction. Shortest straight-line distance measured from the initial position to the final position.
Quantity Type Scalar quantity (has magnitude only). Vector quantity (has both magnitude and specific direction).
Sign & Value Always positive; never zero or negative for a body in motion. Can be positive, negative, or zero.
Closed Loop Motion Equal to total perimeter (\(s = 2\pi r\) for a full circle). Exactly zero (\(\vec{s} = 0\)) because initial and final positions coincide.
SI & CGS Units SI: \(\text{metre (m)}\); CGS: \(\text{centimetre (cm)}\) SI: \(\text{metre (m)}\); CGS: \(\text{centimetre (cm)}\)

3. Speed Formulas & Conversion Derivation

Mathematical Proof: \(1\text{ km/h} = \frac{5}{18}\text{ m/s}\)
1. By definition of SI metric base units:
    \(1\text{ kilometre (km)} = 1,000\text{ metres (m)}\)
    \(1\text{ hour (h)} = 60\text{ minutes} \times 60\text{ seconds} = 3,600\text{ seconds (s)}\)

2. Taking the ratio of length over time:
    $$1\text{ km/h} = \frac{1,000\text{ m}}{3,600\text{ s}} = \frac{10}{36}\text{ m/s} = \frac{\mathbf{5}}{\mathbf{18}}\text{ m/s} \approx 0.2778\text{ m/s}$$
3. Practical Calculation Axiom:
    • To convert \(\text{km/h} \to \text{m/s}\): Multiply by \(\frac{5}{18}\).
    • To convert \(\text{m/s} \to \text{km/h}\): Multiply by \(\frac{18}{5}\).

4. Worked CISCE Exemplar Numericals

Exemplar 1.1: Non-Uniform Round Journey [3 Marks]
A car travels a distance of \(120\text{ km}\) from City A to City B at a uniform speed of \(60\text{ km/h}\), and returns from City B to City A over the identical route at a speed of \(40\text{ km/h}\). Calculate: (i) Total time taken, and (ii) Average speed of the entire journey.
Step-by-Step Examiner Solution:

1. Forward Journey (A to B):
    \(t_1 = \frac{s_1}{v_1} = \frac{120\text{ km}}{60\text{ km/h}} = \mathbf{2.0\text{ hours}}\) [1 Mark]

2. Return Journey (B to A):
    \(t_2 = \frac{s_2}{v_2} = \frac{120\text{ km}}{40\text{ km/h}} = \mathbf{3.0\text{ hours}}\)
    Total Time \(T_{\text{total}} = 2.0 + 3.0 = \mathbf{5.0\text{ hours}}\) [1 Mark]

3. Average Speed:
    Total Distance \(S_{\text{total}} = 120 + 120 = 240\text{ km}\)
    $$v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{S_{\text{total}}}{T_{\text{total}}} = \frac{240\text{ km}}{5\text{ h}} = \mathbf{48\text{ km/h}} \quad \left(= 48 \times \frac{5}{18} = \mathbf{13.33\text{ m/s}}\right)$$ [1 Mark]
Exemplar 1.2: Circular Track Distance & Displacement [3 Marks]
An athlete runs along a circular track of radius \(r = 14\text{ m}\). Calculate the distance covered and the magnitude of displacement when the athlete completes: (i) Half a revolution, (ii) One full revolution. [Take \(\pi = \frac{22}{7}\)]
Step-by-Step Examiner Solution:

1. Case (i): Half a Revolution (Diameter opposite):
    • Distance \(s = \pi r = \frac{22}{7} \times 14\text{ m} = \mathbf{44\text{ m}}\)
    • Displacement \(\vec{s} = \text{Diameter} = 2r = 2 \times 14\text{ m} = \mathbf{28\text{ m}}\) [1.5 Marks]

2. Case (ii): One Full Revolution (Initial = Final position):
    • Distance \(s = 2\pi r = 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 14\text{ m} = \mathbf{88\text{ m}}\)
    • Displacement \(\vec{s} = \mathbf{0\text{ m}}\) (since the athlete returns to the exact starting point) [1.5 Marks]
