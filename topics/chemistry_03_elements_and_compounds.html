Chemistry • Topic 03

Elements, Compounds & Mixtures

The macroscopic and molecular classification of pure matter versus heterogeneous and homogeneous mixtures, the Law of Constant Proportions, and classical separation science.

1. Pure Substances vs. Impure Mixtures

Element: A pure chemical substance composed of only one type of atom that cannot be split into simpler substances by any physical or chemical means (e.g., Gold \(\text{Au}\), Oxygen \(\text{O}_2\), Iron \(\text{Fe}\)).
Compound: A pure substance formed when two or more different elements chemically combine in a strictly fixed proportion by mass (e.g., Water \(\text{H}_2\text{O}\), Carbon Dioxide \(\text{CO}_2\)). The properties of a compound differ entirely from its constituent elements.
Mixture: An impure substance formed by physically blending two or more substances in any arbitrary proportion without any chemical reaction taking place (e.g., Air, Brass, Sea Water).

2. Compounds vs. Mixtures Comparison

Comparison Criteria Compounds Mixtures
Composition Constituents present in a strictly fixed ratio by mass. Constituents mixed in any variable proportion.
Nature of Properties Properties are entirely different from constituent elements. Constituents retain their individual identities and properties.
Separation of Constituents Can be separated only by chemical or electrochemical reactions. Can be separated readily by simple physical methods.
Energy Change During Formation Accompanied by significant absorption or evolution of heat/light. Formed with zero or negligible heat exchange.
Melting & Boiling Points Possess sharp, fixed, characteristic melting and boiling points. Show variable boiling ranges depending on composition.
