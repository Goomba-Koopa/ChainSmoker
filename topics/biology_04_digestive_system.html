Human Digestive System & Nutrition

The 5 phases of holozoic nutrition, alimentary canal anatomy, enzymatic breakdown of carbohydrates, proteins, and lipids, and nutrient absorption by intestinal villi.

1. The Five Stages of Holozoic Nutrition

1. Ingestion: Taking food into the body via the oral buccal cavity.
2. Digestion: Mechanical mastication and enzymatic breakdown of complex, insoluble macromolecules into simple, soluble, diffusible molecules.
3. Absorption: Transport of digested nutrients across the intestinal wall into bloodstream and lymphatic capillaries.
4. Assimilation: Cellular utilization of absorbed nutrients for energy synthesis, tissue repair, and protoplasmic growth.
5. Egestion: Elimination of undigested, unabsorbed solid waste matter from the rectum through the anus.

3. Structural Adaptations of the Small Intestine

Intestinal Villi: Millions of microscopic, finger-like projections lining the inner mucosal wall of the ileum that increase the internal surface area by over 300-fold for rapid nutrient absorption.

• Each villus contains a central lymphatic capillary called a lacteal (which absorbs fatty acids and glycerol) surrounded by a dense network of blood capillaries (which absorb amino acids, glucose, water-soluble vitamins, and minerals directly into the hepatic portal circulation).
