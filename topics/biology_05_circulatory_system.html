Biology • Topic 05

Human Circulatory System, Blood & Heart

The dual-pump physiology of the four-chambered mammalian heart, the cardiac cycle, double circulation pathways, cellular blood components, and hemodynamic distinctions between arteries and veins.

1. Four-Chambered Heart Anatomy & Valves

Chambers of the Human Heart: • Right Atrium (Auricle): Receives deoxygenated blood from the body tissues via the Superior and Inferior Vena Cava.
• Right Ventricle: Pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs via the Pulmonary Artery for oxygenation.
• Left Atrium: Receives freshly oxygenated blood from the lungs via four Pulmonary Veins.
• Left Ventricle: Possesses the thickest muscular myocardium; pumps high-pressure oxygenated blood to the entire systemic body via the main systemic Aorta.
Unidirectional Heart Valves: • Tricuspid Valve: Three cusps guarding the right atrioventricular aperture; prevents backflow into the right atrium.
• Bicuspid (Mitral) Valve: Two cusps guarding the left atrioventricular aperture.
• Semilunar Valves: Half-moon shaped pocket valves at the base of the Pulmonary Artery and Aorta preventing blood from flowing back into the relaxing ventricles.

2. Arteries vs. Veins Hemodynamic Comparison

Comparison Criteria Arteries Veins
Direction of Blood Flow Carry blood away from the heart to body tissues. Carry blood towards the heart from body tissues.
Type of Blood Carried Carry Oxygenated blood (Exception: Pulmonary Artery carries deoxygenated blood). Carry Deoxygenated blood (Exception: Pulmonary Vein carries oxygenated blood).
Vessel Wall Structure Thick, muscular, highly elastic walls; narrow lumen. Thin, non-elastic walls; wide, collapsible lumen.
Internal Valves No internal valves (blood moves under high pulsating pressure). Contain pocket-shaped semilunar valves to prevent backward flow.
Pressure of Flow Blood flows in rapid, pulsating surges under very high pressure. Blood flows in a smooth, steady, continuous stream under low pressure.
