1.0 Classification of Pure Substances & Mixtures

1. Pure Substances (Elements & Compounds)

A material having a uniform and definite chemical composition throughout, with sharp invariant physical constants (fixed melting point, fixed boiling point, definite density).

Examples: Pure Copper (Cu), Distilled Water (Hâ‚‚O), Sodium Chloride (NaCl).

2. Impure Substances (Mixtures)

A physical combination of two or more substances in any variable proportion, where constituent components retain their original individual chemical properties.

Examples: Atmospheric Air, Sea Water, Gunpowder, Brass alloy.

2.0 Master Distinction: Compound vs Mixture (CISCE 5-Mark Scheme)

Comparison Criterion Compound Mixture
1. Proportion of Constituents Fixed, definite proportion by mass (Law of Definite Proportions). Variable proportion; constituents can be mixed in any ratio.
2. Chemical Properties Totally new properties; constituent elements lose their identity. Retains the individual properties of its constituent substances.
3. Method of Separation Cannot be separated by physical methods (chemical/electrolytic only). Readily separated by simple physical methods (magnetism, filtration, distillation).
4. Energy Exchange Significant heat/light energy evolved or absorbed during formation. Zero or negligible heat exchange occurs upon mixing.
5. Melting & Boiling Points Sharp, fixed and constant (e.g. Pure Water BP = 100Â°C). Varies over a broad temperature range.
