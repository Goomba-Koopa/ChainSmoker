Chemistry • Topic 01

Matter & Kinetic Molecular Theory

The particulate nature of matter, the 4 fundamental postulates of Kinetic Molecular Theory, Intermolecular Forces vs Spaces, and the 5-point comparative ledger across Solids, Liquids, and Gases.

1. Postulates of Kinetic Molecular Theory

The 4 Fundamental Kinetic Postulates: 1. Particulate Nature: Matter is composed of minute particles called atoms or molecules which are identical in a pure substance.
2. Intermolecular Space (\(d\)): Constituent molecules are separated by empty spaces called intermolecular or inter-particle spaces.
3. Intermolecular Attraction (\(F\)): Molecules exert mutual forces of attraction on one another (Cohesion between identical molecules; Adhesion between different molecules). This force decreases rapidly as distance increases (\(F \propto \frac{1}{d^2}\)).
4. Continuous Kinetic Motion: Molecules are in a state of ceaseless, random (Brownian) motion and possess kinetic energy directly proportional to absolute temperature (\(E_k \propto T\)).

2. Solids vs. Liquids vs. Gases

Physical Property Solid State Liquid State Gaseous State
Shape & Volume Definite shape and definite volume. Definite volume, but takes the shape of the container. No definite shape and no definite volume.
Intermolecular Space Extremely small (minimum); molecules are tightly packed. Moderate; larger than solids, smaller than gases. Very large (maximum); molecules are far apart.
Intermolecular Force Strongest (maximum); locks particles in fixed positions. Intermediate; allows particles to slide past one another.
Compressibility Incompressible (negligible). Almost incompressible. Highly compressible.
Molecular Motion Only vibrate about their fixed mean positions. Move freely within the boundary of the liquid. Move freely in all directions at high speeds.

3. Phase Change Dynamics

Thermal Energy in State Transitions
• Melting (Fusion): \(\text{Solid} \xrightarrow{+\text{Heat}} \text{Liquid}\). Absorbed thermal energy overcomes strong intermolecular forces without raising temperature (Latent Heat of Fusion).
• Vaporization (Boiling): \(\text{Liquid} \xrightarrow{+\text{Heat}} \text{Gas}\). Molecules acquire sufficient kinetic energy to completely break free from mutual cohesive attraction.
• Sublimation: \(\text{Solid} \xrightarrow{+\text{Heat}} \text{Gas (directly without liquid state)}\). Observed in Ammonium chloride (\(\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}\)), Iodine (\(\text{I}_2\)), Camphor, and Naphthalene.
