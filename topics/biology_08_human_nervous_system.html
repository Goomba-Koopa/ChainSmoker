Human Nervous System, Neuron Anatomy & The Reflex Arc Pathway

Textbook exposition: Neuron structure (Cyton, Dendrites, Axon, Myelin Sheath, Synapse), Division of Nervous System (CNS - Brain & Spinal Cord, PNS, ANS), Three regions of the Human Brain (Cerebrum, Cerebellum, Medulla Oblongata), and Reflex Arc pathway.

1.0 The Three Major Parts of the Human Brain

1. Cerebrum (Forebrain)

Largest part (80% brain volume). Highly folded with gyri and sulci.

Functions: Seat of conscious intelligence, thinking, memory, reasoning, willpower, and sensory perception.

2. Cerebellum (Little Brain)

Located at the posterior base beneath cerebrum; no convolutions.

Functions: Coordinates muscular movements and maintains dynamic body posture & balance.

3. Medulla Oblongata

Lowest stem connecting directly with spinal cord.

Functions: Controls involuntary life-vital actions (heartbeat, breathing rate, peristalsis, sneezing).

2.0 The Five Elements of a Reflex Arc (Automatic Involuntary Action)

Linear Chain of Nerve Impulses:

1. Receptor (Sense Organ): Detects external stimulus (e.g. sharp needle prick on skin).

2. Sensory (Afferent) Neuron: Transmits sensory impulse to the Spinal Cord.

3. Interneuron / Relay Neuron: Located in spinal grey matter; integrates and switches the impulse.

4. Motor (Efferent) Neuron: Transmits rapid motor command from spinal cord to muscle.

5. Effector (Muscle / Gland): Arm biceps muscle contracts instantly to withdraw hand from harm.

Reflex actions are instantaneous and bypass conscious brain processing to ensure biological survival.
