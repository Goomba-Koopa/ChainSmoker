The Balance Beam: Linear Equations & Word Problems

The mechanics of algebraic balance: equations as two-pan scales, why operations flip signs across the equals boundary, clearing denominators via the LCM, and a complete translation dictionary for solving age, consecutive number, and digit puzzles.

  • ✓

The Grade 6 Bridge

From Grade 6 Intuition to Grade 7 Reality

Mathematics builds layer upon layer. Here is how Grade 6 inverse guessing evolves into Grade 7 rigorous transposition:
Grade 6: The Simple Model Single-Step Guess & Check Puzzles
  • Trial and Error: Equations were framed as riddle questions: "what number plus 5 makes 12?" solved by inspection.
  • Single-Step Inverses: Operations were limited to moving one number (\(x + 4 = 11 \implies x = 7\)) without multi-term grouping.
  • Single-Sided Variables: Unknown letters appeared on only one side; fractions were rarely distributed across parentheses.
Grade 7: The Deeper Reality Dual-Pan Transposition & LCM Clearing
  • Dual-Pan Equilibrium: Equality represents an exact balance beam: any operation performed on the LHS must identically alter the RHS.
  • Systematic Transposition: Variables on both sides are gathered systematically: \(ax + b = cx + d \implies (a - c)x = d - b\).
  • LCM Fraction Elimination: Complex fractional linear equations are cleared by multiplying every term by \(\text{LCM}(d_1, d_2, \dots)\) before expanding brackets.

Introduction: The Two-Pan Weighing Scale

Picture an old-fashioned brass scale in a spice bazaar. On the left pan sits a mysterious sealed burlap bag plus two 1-kilogram iron weights. On the right pan sit eight 1-kilogram iron weights.

The pointer in the middle rests perfectly vertical: the two pans are in exact equilibrium.

How heavy is the mysterious bag? You don't need magic to find out. If you take away two 1-kg weights from the left pan, the scale will tilt—unless you also take away two 1-kg weights from the right pan!

Once you remove 2 kg from both sides, the left pan holds only the bag, and the right pan holds \(8 - 2 = 6\text{ kg}\). The mystery is solved: the bag weighs 6 kg!

In mathematics, this is the entire secret of Linear Equations: $$x + 2 = 8 \implies x = 8 - 2 = \mathbf{6}$$ An equation is not a command to calculate; it is a statement that two expressions are in perfect physical balance.

What is a Linear Equation in One Variable? An algebraic statement involving an equality sign (\(=\)) where:
  • There is only one unknown variable (usually \(x\)).
  • The highest power (degree) of the variable is strictly 1 (no \(x^2\), no \(x^3\)).
  • The general form is: $$ax + b = c \quad (\text{where } a \neq 0)$$
The specific number that makes the equation true is called the root or solution of the equation.

1. The Transposition Mechanism: Why Signs Flip

Teachers often say: "When a number hops over the equals sign, its sign flips from plus to minus!"

While that is a handy memory shortcut, it is important to understand why it happens. Nothing is magically flying through the air. You are simply performing the exact same operation to both sides of the balance:

The Four Golden Balances of Transposition
1. Addition flips to Subtraction:
$$x + 7 = 12$$ Subtract 7 from both sides: \(x + 7 - 7 = 12 - 7 \implies \mathbf{x = 12 - 7 = 5}\).

2. Subtraction flips to Addition:
$$x - 9 = 15$$ Add 9 to both sides: \(x - 9 + 9 = 15 + 9 \implies \mathbf{x = 15 + 9 = 24}\).

3. Multiplication flips to Division:
$$4x = 28$$ Divide both sides by 4: \(\frac{4x}{4} = \frac{28}{4} \implies \mathbf{x = 7}\).

4. Division flips to Multiplication:
$$\frac{x}{6} = 5$$ Multiply both sides by 6: \(\frac{x}{6} \times 6 = 5 \times 6 \implies \mathbf{x = 30}\).

2. Taming Fractions: The LCM Clearing Trick

Equations with messy fractions look intimidating. The secret to solving them in seconds is: wipe out all denominators in one clean strike!

The 3-Step LCM Strategy
  1. Step 1: Find the LCM of all denominators across the entire equation.
  2. Step 2: Multiply every single term on both sides of the equation by that LCM. All fractions vanish instantly!
  3. Step 3: Gather all \(x\) terms on the left and all numbers on the right.
Walkthrough: The Cross-Multiplication Shortcut
When an equation is simply one fraction equal to another fraction: $$\frac{ax + b}{c} = \frac{px + q}{d}$$ You can cross-multiply across the equals sign: $$\mathbf{d(ax + b) = c(px + q)}$$ Why it works: Multiplying both sides by the common denominator \(cd\) cancels \(c\) on the left and \(d\) on the right!
Heads-Up • Clearing Denominators Across All Terms

Consider the equation: $$\frac{x}{4} + 3 = 7$$ When multiplying by 4 to clear the fraction, multiply every term on both sides by 4: $$4 \cdot \left(\frac{x}{4}\right) + 4 \cdot 3 = 4 \cdot 7 \implies x + 12 = 28$$

3. Translating English into Algebra: The Word Problem Dictionary

Word problems are not math tests—they are translation tests. You are translating ordinary English sentences into algebraic sentences:

English Clue Phrase Mathematical Meaning Algebraic Expression
"A number increased by 8" Addition (\(+\)) \(x + 8\)
"5 subtracted from a number" Subtraction (\(-\)) \(x - 5\) (Caution: NOT \(5 - x\)!)
"Twice a number exceeds 10 by 4" Difference equals 4 \(2x - 10 = 4\)
"Three consecutive integers" Stepping by 1 each time \(x, \; x + 1, \; x + 2\)
"Three consecutive odd/even numbers" Stepping by 2 each time \(x, \; x + 2, \; x + 4\)
"In 5 years' time (5 years hence)" Future age (+5) \(\text{Age} + 5\)
"4 years ago" Past age (-4) \(\text{Age} - 4\)
"A two-digit number with tens digit \(t\) and units digit \(u\)" Place value expansion \(10t + u\)

Worked Problems

Problem 4.1: Fractional Equation with Nested Signs ICSE Exam Standard • 4 Marks
Solve for \(x\) and verify your answer: $$\frac{3x - 2}{4} - \frac{2x + 3}{3} = \frac{2}{3} - x$$
Step 1: Find the LCM of all denominators:
The denominators are 4, 3, and 3. The \(\text{LCM}(4, 3) = \mathbf{12}\).

Step 2: Multiply every single term by 12:
$$12 \times \left(\frac{3x - 2}{4}\right) - 12 \times \left(\frac{2x + 3}{3}\right) = 12 \times \left(\frac{2}{3}\right) - 12 \times x$$ $$3(3x - 2) - 4(2x + 3) = 4(2) - 12x$$
Step 3: Expand brackets (Watch the negative sign!):
$$9x - 6 - 8x - 12 = 8 - 12x$$ Combine like terms on left:
$$(9x - 8x) + (-6 - 12) = 8 - 12x$$ $$x - 18 = 8 - 12x$$
Step 4: Transpose variables to left and constants to right:
$$x + 12x = 8 + 18$$ $$13x = 26$$ $$x = \frac{26}{13} = \mathbf{2}$$
Step 5: Quick Verification:
• \(\text{LHS} = \frac{3(2) - 2}{4} - \frac{2(2) + 3}{3} = \frac{4}{4} - \frac{7}{3} = 1 - 2\frac{1}{3} = -\frac{4}{3}\).
• \(\text{RHS} = \frac{2}{3} - 2 = -\frac{4}{3}\).
\(\text{LHS} = \text{RHS}\). Verified!
Problem 4.2: Classic Age Modeling Puzzle ICSE Exam Standard • 4 Marks
A father is 30 years older than his son. In 12 years' time, the father will be exactly twice as old as his son will be then. Find their present ages.
Step 1: Define variables for the present day:
Let the son's present age be \(x\) years.
Then the father's present age is \((x + 30)\) years.

Step 2: Move 12 years into the future:
• Son's age in 12 years = \((x + 12)\) years.
• Father's age in 12 years = \((x + 30) + 12 = (x + 42)\) years.

Step 3: Set up the equation using the given condition:
"Father's future age = 2 × Son's future age"
$$x + 42 = 2(x + 12)$$
Step 4: Solve for \(x\):
$$x + 42 = 2x + 24$$ $$42 - 24 = 2x - x$$ $$18 = x \implies \mathbf{x = 18}$$
Conclusion:
• Son's present age = 18 years
• Father's present age = 18 + 30 = 48 years
(Check: In 12 years, son is 30, father is 60. \(60 = 2 \times 30\). Perfect!)
Problem 4.3: Two-Digit Number & Reversal Puzzle ICSE Exam Standard • 4 Marks
The sum of the digits of a two-digit number is 9. If 27 is added to the number, its digits are reversed. Find the original number.
Step 1: Set up the digits:
Let the units digit be \(x\).
Since the sum of digits is 9, the tens digit is \((9 - x)\).

Step 2: Write the place value of the original number:
$$\text{Original Number} = 10 \times (\text{tens digit}) + (\text{units digit})$$ $$= 10(9 - x) + x = 90 - 10x + x = \mathbf{90 - 9x}$$
Step 3: Write the reversed number:
When digits are reversed, \(x\) becomes the tens digit and \((9 - x)\) becomes the units digit:
$$\text{Reversed Number} = 10(x) + (9 - x) = \mathbf{9x + 9}$$
Step 4: Form the equation:
"Original Number + 27 = Reversed Number"
$$(90 - 9x) + 27 = 9x + 9$$ $$117 - 9x = 9x + 9$$
Step 5: Solve for \(x\):
$$117 - 9 = 9x + 9x$$ $$108 = 18x \implies x = \frac{108}{18} = \mathbf{6}$$
Conclusion:
• Units digit = \(6\)
• Tens digit = \(9 - 6 = 3\)
• The Original Number is 36!
(Check: Sum of digits = \(3 + 6 = 9\). Add 27: \(36 + 27 = 63\), which is 36 reversed!)

Mathematical Curiosities & "Why?" Questions

Curiosity 4.1: The Dangerous "Proof" That \(1 = 2\)
Why is dividing both sides of an equation by \((a - b)\) strictly illegal when \(a = b\)?
Look at this famous mathematical fallacy:
1. Let \(a = b\).
2. Multiply both sides by \(a\): \(a^2 = ab\).
3. Subtract \(b^2\) from both sides: \(a^2 - b^2 = ab - b^2\).
4. Factor both sides: \((a - b)(a + b) = b(a - b)\).
5. Divide both sides by \((a - b)\): \(a + b = b\).
6. Since \(a = b\), substitute \(b\) for \(a\): \(b + b = b \implies 2b = b \implies 2 = 1\)!

Where was the crime committed? In Step 5! Since \(a = b\), the term \((a - b) = 0\). Dividing by \((a - b)\) is dividing by zero, which is mathematically catastrophic. That is why in algebra, you can never divide an equation by an expression that might equal zero!
Curiosity 4.2: Why Are They Called "Linear" Equations?
Why does the word "Linear" have the word "Line" inside it?
If you plot the solutions to any degree-1 equation (such as \(y = 2x + 1\)) on a coordinate grid by graphing pairs of \((x, y)\) points, they will always connect to form an unbroken, perfectly straight geometric line.

Higher degree equations curve like rollercoasters (degree 2 curves into a parabola, degree 3 twists into an S-curve). Only degree-1 relationships maintain a constant, steady rate of change, creating a straight line. Hence: **linear**.
Exam Strategy • The 5-Second Filter

8. The Common-Sense Strategy: Rule Out the Impossible

Elimination Drill
"Common sense is the law." A solution to an equation is not an opinion—it is an exact mathematical balance point. Top problem solvers don't blindly compute; they inspect the options first and ask: Which of these roots fail the mental balance test or violate fraction scaling? In multiple-choice questions, you can frequently rule out 2 or 3 options in under 5 seconds.
Check 01 The Substitution Sanity Check

A true root MUST make LHS = RHS identically. If quick mental substitution produces unequal integers or fractions, disqualify that candidate instantly.

Check 02 The Negative Fraction Distribution Trap

When expanding \(-\frac{x - 1}{2}\), multiplying by the LCM produces \(-3(x - 1) = -3x + 3\). Forgetting to flip the inner sign is the #1 ICSE board trap.

Check 03 The Scale & Magnitude Bracket

If the RHS is a modest number like 2, an enormous value like \(x = 17\) or \(x = 35\) will blow the LHS into double digits. Rule out wildly oversized candidates.

B \(x = 1\)

⚡ Ruled Out in 2 Seconds Zero-Numerator Mental Test
Why common sense eliminates this: Test \(x = 1\): The first term is \(\frac{2(1)+1}{3} = \frac{3}{3} = 1\). The second term is \(\frac{1-1}{2} = 0\). \(1 - 0 = 1 \neq 2\). LHS is half of RHS!
C \(x = 13\)
Click to test with common sense ⚡
⚡ Ruled Out in 5 Seconds The Bracket Sign Trap
Why common sense eliminates this: This traps students who write \(-3(x - 1) = -3x - 3\), giving \(x - 1 = 12 \implies x = 13\). Quick substitution confirms failure: \(\frac{27}{3} - \frac{12}{2} = 9 - 6 = 3 \neq 2\)!
D \(x = 7\)
Click to test with common sense ⚡
✓ The Only Survivor! Physical & Mathematical Confirmation
Why common sense picks this without calculating: Quick check: \(\frac{2(7)+1}{3} = \frac{15}{3} = 5\). \(\frac{7-1}{2} = \frac{6}{2} = 3\). \(5 - 3 = 2 = \text{RHS}\). Exact physical equilibrium!

The 10-Second Mathematical Proof:
• \(\text{LCM}(3, 2) = 6\). Multiply every single term by 6:
  \(2(2x + 1) - 3(x - 1) = 6 \times 2\).
• Expand carefully: \(4x + 2 - 3x + 3 = 12\).
• Combine like terms: \(x + 5 = 12 \implies \mathbf{x = 7}\).
