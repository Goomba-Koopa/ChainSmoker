Electricity, Electric Current & Circuits

The rate of flow of charge (\(I = Q/t\)), Series vs Parallel circuit topologies, Joule heating mechanics, and electric fuse safety alloys.

1. Electric Current & Units

Definition of Electric Current (\(I\)): Electric current is defined as the rate of flow of electric charge (\(Q\)) across any cross-section of a conductor: $$I = \frac{Q}{t}$$
• SI Unit: \(\text{Ampere (A)} = \frac{1\text{ Coulomb (C)}}{1\text{ second (s)}}\).
• 1 Ampere Definition: Current through a conductor is \(1\text{ A}\) when a net charge of \(1\text{ Coulomb}\) flows through it in \(1\text{ second}\) (\(1\text{ C} \approx 6.25 \times 10^{18}\text{ electrons}\)).

2. Series vs. Parallel Circuit Arrangements

Characteristic Series Circuit Parallel Circuit
Pathway for Current Only one single continuous path for current flow. Multiple branching paths for current to divide.
Current through Components Identical current passes through every resistor (\(I_1 = I_2 = I\)). Current divides inversely with resistance (\(I = I_1 + I_2\)).
Potential Difference (Voltage) Total voltage divides: \(V = V_1 + V_2 + V_3\). Equal voltage across all branches (\(V_1 = V_2 = V\)).
Failure of One Appliance Entire circuit breaks; all other appliances turn OFF. Other branches continue working independently.
Application Decorative holiday fairy lights. Household domestic wiring.

3. Heating Effect & Electric Fuse Safety

Alloy Properties of Electric Fuse Wire
An electric fuse is a crucial safety device placed in series with the Live Wire to protect against short circuits and overloads.

• Composition: Alloy of Lead (\(63\%\)) and Tin (\(37\%\)).
• Crucial Properties:
    1. Low Melting Point: \(\approx 200^\circ\text{C}\) so it rapidly melts when current exceeds safe limits.
    2. High Specific Resistance: Produces rapid Joule heating (\(H = I^2 R t\)) when overloaded.

4. Worked CISCE Exemplar Numerical

Exemplar 5.1: Rate of Charge Flow [3 Marks]
A charge of \(360\text{ Coulombs}\) passes through an electric lamp filament in \(5\text{ minutes}\). Calculate: (i) The electric current flowing through the filament in Amperes, (ii) The total number of electrons that pass through the filament in this time. [Charge on 1 electron \(e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}\)]
Step-by-Step Examiner Solution:

1. Current Calculation:
    Time in seconds \(t = 5 \times 60 = 300\text{ s}\)
    $$I = \frac{Q}{t} = \frac{360\text{ C}}{300\text{ s}} = \mathbf{1.2\text{ A}}$$ [1.5 Marks]

2. Number of Electrons Transferred:
    $$n = \frac{Q}{e} = \frac{360\text{ C}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}} = \mathbf{2.25 \times 10^{21}\text{ electrons}}$$ [1.5 Marks]
