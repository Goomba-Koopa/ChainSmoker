Physics • Topic 07

Heat Energy, Temperature & Thermometry

Total kinetic energy of molecules vs thermal state (Temperature), thermometric scales derivation (\(\frac{C}{5} = \frac{F-32}{9} = \frac{K-273}{5}\)), and anomalous expansion of water.

1. Heat Energy vs. Temperature

Characteristic Heat Energy (\(Q\)) Temperature (\(T\))
Definition Form of energy transferred between systems due to a temperature gradient. Degree of hotness or coldness of a body; measures average molecular kinetic energy.
Molecular Basis Total kinetic energy of all constituent molecules in the body. Average kinetic energy per molecule of the body.
Flow Direction Always flows spontaneously from a body at higher temperature to one at lower temperature. Determines the direction of heat flow when two bodies are placed in thermal contact.
SI & Practical Units SI: Joule (\(\text{J}\)); Practical: \(\text{calorie (cal)} = 4.186\text{ J}\). SI: Kelvin (\(\text{K}\)); Practical: Degree Celsius (\(^\circ\text{C}\)), Fahrenheit (\(^\circ\text{F}\)).

2. Derivation of Thermometric Conversion Scales

Fundamental Thermometric Relation
For any linear temperature scale, the ratio of reading minus Lower Fixed Point (LFP: melting ice) to the fundamental interval (UFP - LFP) is constant: $$\frac{\text{Reading} - \text{LFP}}{\text{UFP} - \text{LFP}} = \text{Constant}$$
• Celsius Scale: \(\text{LFP} = 0^\circ\text{C}, \text{UFP} = 100^\circ\text{C} \implies \frac{C - 0}{100} = \frac{C}{100}\)
• Fahrenheit Scale: \(\text{LFP} = 32^\circ\text{F}, \text{UFP} = 212^\circ\text{F} \implies \frac{F - 32}{180}\)
• Kelvin Scale: \(\text{LFP} = 273\text{ K}, \text{UFP} = 373\text{ K} \implies \frac{K - 273}{100}\)

Equating and dividing by 20 gives the Master Formula: $$\frac{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{5}} = \frac{\mathbf{F - 32}}{\mathbf{9}} = \frac{\mathbf{K - 273}}{\mathbf{5}}$$

3. Anomalous Expansion of Water (\(0^\circ\text{C}\) to \(4^\circ\text{C}\))

Anomalous Behavior: Almost all liquids expand continuously when heated. Water exhibits exceptional behavior: when heated from \(0^\circ\text{C}\) to \(4^\circ\text{C}\), it contracts instead of expanding. Its volume becomes minimum and its density becomes maximum (\(1000\text{ kg/m}^3\)) at exactly \(4^\circ\text{C}\).

• Biological Significance in Cold Climates: In freezing winters, surface water cools to \(0^\circ\text{C}\) and forms an insulating sheet of ice. The heavier, denser water at \(4^\circ\text{C}\) sinks to the bottom of the lake or pond, preserving marine and aquatic life beneath the frozen crust.

4. Worked CISCE Exemplar Numerical

Exemplar 7.1: Identical Temperature Scale Point [3 Marks]
Find the temperature at which the reading on the Fahrenheit scale is numerically equal to the reading on the Celsius scale.
Step-by-Step Examiner Solution:

1. Let the common temperature value be \(x\):
    \(C = F = x\) [1 Mark]

2. Substitute into the thermometric equation:
    $$\frac{x}{5} = \frac{x - 32}{9}$$
    $$9x = 5(x - 32)$$
    $$9x = 5x - 160$$
    $$4x = -160 \implies x = \mathbf{-40}$$ [1 Mark]

3. Final Result Statement:
    \(\mathbf{-40^\circ\text{C} = -40^\circ\text{F}}\) (The two scales read identically at \(-40^\circ\)). [1 Mark]
