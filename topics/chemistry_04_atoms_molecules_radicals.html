Chemistry • Topic 04

Atoms, Molecules & Chemical Radicals

The distinction between atoms and molecules, atomicity classifications, basic electropositive radicals (+1 to +4), acidic electronegative radicals (-1 to -4), and criss-cross formula construction.

1. Atom vs. Molecule

Comparison Criteria Atom Molecule
Definition Smallest particle of an element that can take part in a chemical reaction. Smallest particle of an element or compound capable of independent existence.
Independent Existence Usually cannot exist independently in free state (except noble gases). Capable of free, stable independent existence in nature.
Constituents Composed of fundamental subatomic particles (protons, neutrons, electrons). Composed of two or more chemically bonded atoms (same or different elements).
Examples \(\text{H}, \text{O}, \text{Na}, \text{Fe}, \text{He}\) \(\text{H}_2, \text{O}_2, \text{O}_3, \text{H}_2\text{O}, \text{CO}_2, \text{CH}_4\)

2. Chemical Radicals & Valency Ledger

Chemical Radical: An atom or group of chemically bonded atoms of different elements that behaves as a single unit with a net positive or negative electric charge in chemical reactions.
Valency Charge Basic (Electropositive) Cations Acidic (Electronegative) Anions
Monovalent (\(\pm 1\)) \(\text{K}^+\), \(\text{Na}^+\), \(\text{H}^+\), \(\text{NH}_4^+\) (Ammonium), \(\text{Cu}^+\) (Cuprous), \(\text{Ag}^+\) \(\text{Cl}^-\) (Chloride), \(\text{OH}^-\) (Hydroxide), \(\text{NO}_3^-\) (Nitrate), \(\text{HCO}_3^-\) (Bicarbonate)
Divalent (\(\pm 2\)) \(\text{Mg}^{2+}\), \(\text{Ca}^{2+}\), \(\text{Zn}^{2+}\), \(\text{Fe}^{2+}\) (Ferrous), \(\text{Cu}^{2+}\) (Cupric), \(\text{Pb}^{2+}\) \(\text{O}^{2-}\) (Oxide), \(\text{S}^{2-}\) (Sulphide), \(\text{SO}_4^{2-}\) (Sulphate), \(\text{CO}_3^{2-}\) (Carbonate)
Trivalent (\(\pm 3\)) \(\text{Al}^{3+}\) (Aluminium), \(\text{Fe}^{3+}\) (Ferric), \(\text{Cr}^{3+}\) \(\text{N}^{3-}\) (Nitride), \(\text{PO}_4^{3-}\) (Phosphate)
Tetravalent (\(\pm 4\)) \(\text{Pb}^{4+}\) (Plumbic), \(\text{Sn}^{4+}\) (Stannic), \(\text{Pt}^{4+}\) \(\text{C}^{4-}\) (Carbide)

3. The Criss-Cross Formula Method

Step-by-Step Chemical Formula Derivation
1. Write the symbols of the positive radical (left) and negative radical (right).
2. Write the valency of each radical at its top right.
3. Criss-cross the valency numbers (ignoring charges) to the bottom right of the partner radical.
4. Simplify the ratio of subscripts if divisible by a common factor.

• Example 1: Aluminium Sulphate:
    Radicals: \(\text{Al}^{3+}\) and \(\text{SO}_4^{2-} \implies \mathbf{Al_2(SO_4)_3}\)

• Example 2: Calcium Carbonate:
    Radicals: \(\text{Ca}^{2+}\) and \(\text{CO}_3^{2-} \implies \text{Ca}_2(\text{CO}_3)_2 \xrightarrow{\text{simplify}} \mathbf{CaCO_3}\)
