Biology • Topic 08

Human Nervous System, Brain & Reflex Arc

The structural and functional architecture of neurons, anatomical divisions of the brain (Cerebrum, Cerebellum, Medulla Oblongata), and the rapid automatic involuntary Reflex Arc pathway.

1. The Neuron: Structural Unit of the Nervous System

Neuron Cytology: • Cyton (Cell Body / Soma): Central nucleated mass containing abundant neuroplasm, granular Nissl's granules, and a prominent nucleus.
• Dendrites: Fine, highly branched, tapering cytoplasmic extensions that receive chemical stimuli from receptors or preceding neurons and conduct electro-chemical nerve impulses towards the cyton.
• Axon: A single, long, cylindrical fiber that transmits electrical impulses away from the cyton to terminal synaptic knobs. Encased in an insulating fatty Myelin Sheath interrupted at intervals by Nodes of Ranvier for saltatory conduction.
• Synapse: The sub-microscopic junction between the axon terminal of one neuron and the dendrites of the adjacent neuron, across which impulses jump via neurotransmitter chemicals (e.g., Acetylcholine).

2. Functional Regions of the Human Brain

Brain Division Anatomical Location Primary Physiological Functions
Cerebrum Largest upper part (Forebrain); highly folded into convolutions (gyri and sulci). Seat of intelligence, conscious thought, memory, reasoning, willpower, emotions, and sensory perception (vision, hearing, smell).
Cerebellum Smaller egg-shaped region located beneath the cerebrum at the back of the skull. Coordinates voluntary muscular movements, maintains posture, dynamic equilibrium, and bodily balance.
Medulla Oblongata Lowest stalk-like part of the brain stem, continuing down as the spinal cord. Controls vital involuntary activities: heartbeat rate, breathing rhythm, blood pressure, coughing, sneezing, and peristalsis.

3. The Reflex Arc: 5-Step Involuntary Pathway

Reflex Action: A rapid, automatic, involuntary response of the body to an external stimulus, mediated directly by the spinal cord without the conscious intervention of the brain (e.g., withdrawing a hand immediately upon touching a boiling hot kettle).

Sequential Path of a Nerve Impulse:
1. Receptor Organ (Skin/Eye): Detects the physical stimulus (e.g., intense heat).
2. Sensory (Afferent) Neuron: Transmits the electrical nerve impulse from the receptor to the dorsal horn of the spinal cord.
3. Interneuron (Relay / Association Neuron): Processes the incoming signal inside the spinal gray matter and routes it to a motor neuron.
4. Motor (Efferent) Neuron: Conducts the response command from the ventral horn of the spinal cord to the effector organ.
5. Effector Organ (Muscle / Gland): Contracts (e.g., biceps muscle flexes) to pull the hand away to safety.
