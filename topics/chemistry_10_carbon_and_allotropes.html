Chemistry • Topic 10

Carbon & Its Allotropic Modifications

The phenomenon of allotropy, comparative crystal architectures (Diamond vs Graphite vs Fullerenes), and amorphous forms (Charcoal, Lampblack, Coal).

1. The Phenomenon of Allotropy

Allotropy: The property by virtue of which an element can exist in two or more different physical forms in the same physical state having different physical properties but identical chemical properties.

2. Diamond vs. Graphite

Property Diamond Graphite
Crystal Architecture Rigid 3D tetrahedral network; each carbon bonded to 4 other carbons. 2D planar hexagonal layers; layers held by weak van der Waals forces.
Hardness & Density Hardest natural substance known; density \(\approx 3.51\text{ g/cm}^3\). Soft, slippery, soapy to touch; density \(\approx 2.25\text{ g/cm}^3\).
Electrical Conductivity Complete non-conductor (all 4 valence electrons locked in bonds). Good electrical conductor (contains free mobile \(\pi\)-electrons).
ICSE Applications Glass cutting, rock drilling, gem jewellery. Pencil leads, high-temp solid lubricant, dry cell battery electrodes.
