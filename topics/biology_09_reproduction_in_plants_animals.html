Biology • Topic 09

Reproduction in Plants & Animals

The continuity of life, asexual vs sexual reproductive modes, anatomy of a complete flower, Self vs Cross pollination, and post-fertilization embryology.

1. Asexual vs. Sexual Reproduction

Characteristic Asexual Reproduction Sexual Reproduction
Number of Parents Uniparental (involves only one single parent organism). Biparental (involves two parents of opposite sexes).
Gamete Formation No gametes or sex cells formed; no fusion. Gametes formed (Sperm/Pollen and Ovum/Egg); fertilization occurs.
Genetic Variation Offspring are genetically identical clones of the parent. Offspring inherit mixed traits, creating genetic variation.
ICSE Modes / Examples Binary Fission (Amoeba), Budding (Hydra/Yeast), Spore Formation. Flowering angiosperms, mammals, reptiles, birds, humans.

2. The Four Whorls of a Flower

Floral Anatomy & Reproductive Organs: • 1. Calyx (Sepals): Outermost green whorl; protects the delicate floral bud prior to opening.
• 2. Corolla (Petals): Brightly coloured, fragrant whorl; attracts insect pollinators.
• 3. Androecium (Male Whorl): Composed of stamens (Filament + Anther producing haploid pollen grains).
• 4. Gynoecium / Pistil (Female Whorl): Composed of carpels (Stigma for pollen reception, Style, and basal Ovary containing ovules).

3. Self-Pollination vs. Cross-Pollination

Comparison Criteria Self-Pollination (Autogamy) Cross-Pollination (Allogamy)
Transfer Vector Pollen transferred from anther to stigma of the same flower or same plant. Pollen transferred to stigma of a different plant of the same species.
External Agents Does not require external pollinating agents (wind, water, insects). Requires external agents (Insects, Wind, Water, Birds).
Genetic Vigor Preserves pure parental lines; continuous selfing may lead to loss of vigor. Produces healthier offspring with superior adaptability and disease resistance.
