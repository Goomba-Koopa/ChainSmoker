Triangles, Congruence, Perimeter & Area

Structural rigidity, angle sum theorems, triangle inequalities, the four congruence criteria, and 2D mensuration boundaries.

1. The Architecture of Triangles: Angle Sum & Exterior Theorems

Have you ever noticed that railway bridges, bicycle frames, and construction cranes are packed with triangles? If you build a four-sided wooden square and push the corner, it immediately collapses into a crooked rhombus. But build a wooden triangle and push any vertex—it refuses to deform. The triangle is the only polygon with inherent geometric rigidity.

Classification of Triangles • By Sides: Scalene (all sides unequal), Isosceles (two sides equal, opposite base angles equal), Equilateral (all three sides equal, each angle \(60^\circ\)).
• By Angles: Acute-angled (all angles \(< 90^\circ\)), Right-angled (one angle \(= 90^\circ\)), Obtuse-angled (one angle \(> 90^\circ\)).
Proof: The Angle Sum Property of a Triangle
Consider \(\triangle ABC\). Through vertex \(A\), draw an auxiliary construction line \(XY\) parallel to base \(BC\).
Because \(XY \parallel BC\) with transversal \(AB\), the alternate interior angles are equal: $$\angle XAB = \angle B$$ With transversal \(AC\), the alternate interior angles are equal: $$\angle YAC = \angle C$$
Observe the straight line \(XAY\). The sum of angles on a straight line is \(180^\circ\): $$\angle XAB + \angle A + \angle YAC = 180^\circ$$ Substituting the alternate angles: $$\mathbf{\angle B + \angle A + \angle C = 180^\circ} \quad \text{(Q.E.D.)}$$
The Exterior Angle Theorem
If side \(BC\) of \(\triangle ABC\) is extended to point \(D\), the angle \(\angle ACD\) is an exterior angle.
From the linear pair on line \(BCD\): $$\angle ACB + \angle ACD = 180^\circ \implies \angle ACD = 180^\circ - \angle ACB$$ From the angle sum of \(\triangle ABC\): $$\angle A + \angle B + \angle ACB = 180^\circ \implies \angle A + \angle B = 180^\circ - \angle ACB$$ Equating both expressions: $$\mathbf{\text{Exterior } \angle ACD = \angle A + \angle B}$$ An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of its two interior opposite angles.
Heads-Up • The Triangle Inequality Theorem

The sum of any two side lengths of a triangle must strictly exceed the third side length (\(a + b > c\)). For side lengths \(3\text{ cm}\), \(4\text{ cm}\), and \(8\text{ cm}\): $$3 + 4 = 7 < 8$$ Since \(7 < 8\), these segments cannot form a closed triangle.

2. Congruence: The Science of Exact Superimposition

In everyday language, "similar" and "equal" are used loosely. In Euclidean geometry, Congruence (\(\cong\)) has a rigorous, unforgiving definition: two figures are congruent if and only if they have the exact same shape and exact same size, such that one can be superimposed onto the other to cover it completely.

CPCTC: Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles

If \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF\), then all 6 corresponding parts match perfectly:

Sides: \(AB = DE, \quad BC = EF, \quad AC = DF\)

Angles: \(\angle A = \angle D, \quad \angle B = \angle E, \quad \angle C = \angle F\)

Once congruence is proven using just 3 elements, all remaining corresponding parts are automatically equal by CPCTC.

Congruence Criterion Required Condition Crucial Geometric Requirement
SSS (Side-Side-Side) All 3 corresponding sides are equal. Three pairs of matching sides: \(AB=DE, BC=EF, AC=DF\).
SAS (Side-Angle-Side) Two sides and the included angle are equal. The angle MUST sit directly between the two known sides (e.g. angle formed at the vertex joining the two sides).
ASA / AAS (Angle-Side-Angle) Two angles and any corresponding side are equal. In ASA, the side is included between the two angles. In AAS, it is non-included. Both guarantee full congruence because the third angle is fixed by the \(180^\circ\) rule.
RHS (Right-Hypotenuse-Side) Right angle, Hypotenuse, and one corresponding leg are equal. Strictly applies to right-angled triangles; the hypotenuses MUST be equal.
Heads-Up • Valid vs Invalid Congruence Criteria

1. AAA is a similarity criterion, not congruence: Equal corresponding angles ensure identical shape, but not necessarily equal scale or side lengths.
2. SSA (Side-Side-Angle) is not a congruence criterion: Specifying two sides and a non-included angle can yield two non-congruent triangles (the ambiguous case).

3. Mensuration: 2D Perimeter & Area Foundations

While geometry studies properties and proofs, Mensuration computes quantitative bounds: the boundary wire length (Perimeter) and the surface tiling capacity (Area).

Geometric Figure Perimeter Formula (Linear Units) Area Formula (Square Units)
Rectangle $$P = 2(l + b)$$ $$A = l \times b$$
Square $$P = 4s$$ $$A = s^2 = \frac{1}{2}d^2 \quad (d = \text{diagonal})$$
Triangle $$P = a + b + c$$ $$A = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \frac{1}{2}bh$$
Parallelogram $$P = 2(a + b)$$ $$A = \text{base} \times \text{height} = bh$$
Circle $$\text{Circumference } C = 2\pi r = \pi d$$ $$A = \pi r^2 \quad \left(\pi \approx \frac{22}{7}\right)$$
Heads-Up • Perpendicular Altitude in Area Calculations

In the area formula \(A = \frac{1}{2}bh\), the height \(h\) must strictly be the perpendicular altitude drawn from the opposing vertex to the base line. Slant heights or adjacent sides cannot be used directly unless the triangle is right-angled.

4. Worked Problem ICSE Examination Problems

Problem 8.1 • Angle Sum & Exterior Property Finding Unknown Angles of a Triangle
In \(\triangle ABC\), the exterior angle at vertex \(C\) measures 125°. If the two interior opposite angles \(\angle A\) and \(\angle B\) are in the ratio 2 : 3, determine the exact measure of all three interior angles of the triangle.
Step 1: Express interior opposite angles in terms of common variable \(x\).
Let \(\angle A = 2x\) and \(\angle B = 3x\).
Step 2: Apply the Exterior Angle Theorem.
$$\text{Exterior } \angle = \angle A + \angle B$$ $$125^\circ = 2x + 3x \implies 5x = 125^\circ \implies x = \frac{125^\circ}{5} = 25^\circ$$
Step 3: Calculate the individual angles.
$$\angle A = 2(25^\circ) = 50^\circ$$ $$\angle B = 3(25^\circ) = 75^\circ$$
Step 4: Find interior \(\angle C\) using Linear Pair or Angle Sum.
$$\angle C = 180^\circ - 125^\circ = 55^\circ$$
Verification: \(50^\circ + 75^\circ + 55^\circ = 180^\circ\). Perfect balance.
Final Answer: \(\angle A = 50^\circ\), \(\angle B = 75^\circ\), and \(\angle C = 55^\circ\).
Problem 8.2 • Geometric Congruence Proof Proving Triangle Congruence via SAS
In a quadrilateral \(ABCD\), diagonal \(AC\) bisects \(\angle A\) such that \(\angle DAC = \angle BAC\). If side \(AD = AB\), prove that: (i) \(\triangle DAC \cong \triangle BAC\), and (ii) side \(DC = BC\).
Step 1: Identify the three corresponding conditions in \(\triangle DAC\) and \(\triangle BAC\).
1. \(AD = AB\) (Given)
2. \(\angle DAC = \angle BAC\) (Given, as \(AC\) bisects \(\angle A\))
3. \(AC = AC\) (Common side shared by both triangles)
Step 2: Conclude congruence by Criterion.
The two matching sides enclose the equal angle (SAS criterion):
$$\mathbf{\triangle DAC \cong \triangle BAC \quad \text{[By SAS Congruence Rule]}}$$
Step 3: Deduce equality of target sides.
Since the triangles are congruent, their corresponding parts match:
$$\mathbf{DC = BC \quad \text{[By CPCTC]}}$$
Q.E.D. The proof is complete with formal geometric rigor.
Problem 8.3 • Mensuration: Running Track / Path Area Calculating the Area of an Enclosing Verandah
A rectangular hall measures 24 m long and 16 m wide. A verandah of uniform width 2 m is constructed all along the outside of the hall. Find the area of the verandah and the cost of paving it at Rs 50 per square meter.
Step 1: Compute inner hall area.
$$\text{Inner Area } A_1 = 24\text{ m} \times 16\text{ m} = 384\text{ m}^2$$
Step 2: Determine outer dimensions including the 2m path on both sides.
$$\text{Outer Length } L = 24 + 2(2) = 24 + 4 = 28\text{ m}$$ $$\text{Outer Breadth } B = 16 + 2(2) = 16 + 4 = 20\text{ m}$$
Step 3: Compute total outer area.
$$\text{Outer Area } A_2 = 28\text{ m} \times 20\text{ m} = 560\text{ m}^2$$
Step 4: Subtract inner area to isolate verandah.
$$\text{Area of Verandah} = A_2 - A_1 = 560 - 384 = 176\text{ m}^2$$
Step 5: Calculate total paving cost.
$$\text{Total Cost} = 176\text{ m}^2 \times Rs\; 50/\text{m}^2 = Rs\; 8,800$$
Final Answer: Verandah Area is 176 m²; Total Paving Cost is Rs 8,800.

5. Structural & Engineering Reasoning

Curiosity 8.1: Why Are Structural Trusses Triangulated?
Why do architectural trusses on bridges, roof rafters, and Eiffel Tower struts use interlocking triangles instead of squares or rectangles?
This stems directly from the SSS Congruence Theorem.

If you pin three solid rods together at their ends to form a triangle, the lengths of all three sides are permanently fixed. By SSS congruence, there is only ONE unique triangle possible—its internal angles cannot change without physically bending or snapping the steel rods! A four-bar linkage (square), however, has flexible angles that allow it to shear into a skewed rhombus without altering side lengths. Triangulation guarantees structural immobility.
Curiosity 8.2: The Iso-Perimetric Paradox: Circle vs Square
If you have a fixed 44 cm loop of string, which geometric shape encloses the maximum possible surface area?
Let perimeter \(P = 44\text{ cm}\):
• Form a Square (side \(s = 11\text{ cm}\)): \(\text{Area} = 11^2 = 121\text{ cm}^2\).
• Form an Equilateral Triangle (side \(s = 14.67\text{ cm}\)): \(\text{Area} \approx 93.1\text{ cm}^2\).
• Form a Circle (\(2\pi r = 44 \implies r = 7\text{ cm}\)): \(\text{Area} = \frac{22}{7} \times 7^2 = 154\text{ cm}^2\).

The circle encloses the greatest area for any given perimeter. This is why water droplets, soap bubbles, and planetary bodies pull themselves into circular/spherical geometry under surface tension and gravity.
