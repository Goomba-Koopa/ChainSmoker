Density, Relative Density & Buoyancy

Density (\(d = m/V\)), Relative Density (\(\text{RD} = \rho / \rho_{\text{water}}\)), Archimedes' Principle (upthrust = weight of fluid displaced), and the Law of Floatation.

The Grade 6 Bridge

From Grade 6 Intuition to Grade 7 Reality

Physics builds layer upon layer. Here is how what you learned in Grade 6 evolves into Grade 7 buoyancy and density:
Grade 6: The Simple Model Intuitive weight and simple sinking vs floating
  • Intuitive Weight: Heavy things sink in water, while light things float.
  • Push-Back Force: Upthrust is just water pushing back because you push down on it.
  • Synonymous Terms: Density and heaviness/weight mean roughly the same thing.
Grade 7: The Deeper Reality Density ratios, Archimedean upthrust, and intensive properties
  • Density Ratio: Flotation depends solely on relative density (\(\rho_{\text{body}} \gtrless \rho_{\text{fluid}}\)), not total mass (a 50,000-tonne steel ship floats).
  • Archimedean Upthrust: Buoyant force equals precisely the weight of displaced liquid (\(F_B = V_{\text{sub}} \rho_L g\)), originating from vertical hydrostatic pressure differentials.
  • Intensive vs Extensive: Density is an intensive material property (\(\rho = m/V\)) independent of sample size, whereas weight is an extensive gravitational force.

1. Concept of Density

Why does a huge block of wood float gracefully on a lake, while a tiny iron nail sinks straight to the bottom? It all comes down to how tightly packed the matter is inside the object. Density is the scientific measure of this compactness, telling us exactly how much mass is squeezed into a specific amount of space. A material with tightly packed particles is highly dense, while one with spread-out particles is less dense.

Density (\(d\) or \(\rho\)): Density is formally defined as the mass per unit volume of a substance. It is a characteristic physical property of a material at a given temperature and pressure. It indicates how heavy a substance is for a particular size.

• Crucial Examiner Insight: Density is independent of the size or shape of the object. A tiny splinter of pine wood has the exact same density as a massive log of the same pine wood.
Mathematical Formula & Units of Density
1. The formula for density relates mass (\(m\)) and volume (\(V\)):
    $$d = \frac{m}{V}$$
2. SI Unit of Density:
    Since mass is in kilograms (\(\text{kg}\)) and volume is in cubic metres (\(\text{m}^3\)), the SI unit is **\(\text{kg/m}^3\)** (kilograms per cubic metre).

3. CGS Unit of Density:
    In smaller measurements, mass is in grams (\(\text{g}\)) and volume in cubic centimetres (\(\text{cm}^3\)). The unit is **\(\text{g/cm}^3\)**.

4. Conversion Identity:
    To convert \(\text{g/cm}^3 \to \text{kg/m}^3\): Multiply by \(1,000\).
    Example: Density of water is \(1 \text{ g/cm}^3 = 1,000 \text{ kg/m}^3\).

2. Relative Density (Specific Gravity)

Comparing substances directly to water makes it incredibly easy to predict if they will float or sink. Instead of memorising large numbers and dealing with complex units, we just look at a simple ratio compared to water's density. This unitless number quickly tells us a material's comparative heaviness.

Relative Density (RD): The relative density of a substance is the ratio of its density to the density of water at \(4^\circ\text{C}\). Because it is a ratio of two identical physical quantities, it is a pure number and has no units whatsoever.
Characteristic Density (\(d\)) Relative Density (RD)
Definition Mass per unit volume of a given substance. Ratio of the substance's density to the density of water at \(4^\circ\text{C}\).
Formula \(d = \frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Volume}}\) \(\text{RD} = \frac{\text{Density of substance}}{\text{Density of water}}\)
Units Has units: \(\text{kg/m}^3\) (SI) or \(\text{g/cm}^3\) (CGS). No units. It is a pure dimensionless scalar.
Value Example Iron: \(7800 \text{ kg/m}^3\) or \(7.8 \text{ g/cm}^3\) Iron: simply \(7.8\)

3. Archimedes' Principle & Upthrust

Have you ever noticed that you feel much lighter when swimming in a pool, or tried to push a plastic ball underwater and felt it fiercely push back? This mysterious upward force is called upthrust or buoyant force. It happens because fluids (liquids and gases) exert pressure on any object placed inside them, pushing them upwards against gravity.

Archimedes' Principle: When a body is completely or partially immersed in a fluid at rest, it experiences an upward buoyant force (upthrust). The magnitude of this upthrust is exactly equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by the immersed part of the body.
Upthrust (Buoyancy) Formula
1. Upthrust (\(F_B\)) = Weight of the displaced fluid.

2. Since Weight = mass \(\times\) gravity, and mass = Volume \(\times\) density:
    $$F_B = V_{\text{submerged}} \times \rho_{\text{fluid}} \times g$$
    Where:
    • \(V_{\text{submerged}}\) = volume of the object that is underwater (\(\text{m}^3\))
    • \(\rho_{\text{fluid}}\) = density of the liquid or gas (\(\text{kg/m}^3\))
    • \(g\) = acceleration due to gravity (approx. \(9.8 \text{ m/s}^2\) or \(10 \text{ m/s}^2\))

3. Apparent Weight of an object in fluid = True Weight in Air \(-\) Upthrust.

4. The Law of Floatation

A massive steel ship can gracefully float on the ocean, even though a solid chunk of steel sinks instantly. This happens because the ship's cleverly designed hollow shape allows it to displace a huge volume of water. This creates an enormous upward buoyant force that perfectly balances its massive weight, allowing it to float.

Law of Floatation: A floating body displaces its own total weight of the fluid in which it floats. For an object to float, the upthrust exerted by the fluid must be perfectly equal to the true weight of the object.
Condition of Densities Comparison of Forces Resulting Action (Will it float?)
Density of object > Density of fluid Weight of object > Maximum Upthrust Sinks to the bottom (e.g., iron in water).
Density of object = Density of fluid Weight of object = Maximum Upthrust Floats fully submerged just below the surface (neutral buoyancy).
Density of object < Density of fluid Weight of object = Upthrust (while only partially submerged) Floats partially above the surface (e.g., cork or ice in water).

5. Worked Problems

Problem 8.1: Calculating Density & Floatation [3 Marks]
A block of metal has a mass of \(540\text{ g}\) and a volume of \(200\text{ cm}^3\). Calculate: (i) its density in \(\text{g/cm}^3\), (ii) its density in \(\text{kg/m}^3\), (iii) will it float or sink in pure water? (Density of water = \(1\text{ g/cm}^3\))
Step-by-Step Examiner Solution:

1. Part (i): Density in \(\text{g/cm}^3\)
    Given: \(m = 540\text{ g}\), \(V = 200\text{ cm}^3\)
    $$d = \frac{m}{V} = \frac{540}{200} = \mathbf{2.7\text{ g/cm}^3}$$ [1 Mark]

2. Part (ii): Density in \(\text{kg/m}^3\)
    To convert \(\text{g/cm}^3\) to \(\text{kg/m}^3\), multiply by \(1000\).
    $$2.7 \times 1000 = \mathbf{2700\text{ kg/m}^3}$$ [1 Mark]

3. Part (iii): Float or Sink condition
    Density of the metal (\(2.7\text{ g/cm}^3\)) is greater than the density of water (\(1.0\text{ g/cm}^3\)).
    Therefore, the block will sink in water. [1 Mark]
Problem 8.2: Archimedes' Upthrust & Volume [4 Marks]
A solid object weighs \(80\text{ N}\) in air and \(65\text{ N}\) when fully submerged in water. Calculate: (i) the upthrust on the solid, and (ii) the volume of the solid. (Take \(\rho_{\text{water}} = 1000\text{ kg/m}^3\) and \(g = 10\text{ m/s}^2\))
Step-by-Step Examiner Solution:

1. Part (i): Upthrust Calculation
    True weight in air (\(W_1\)) = \(80\text{ N}\)
    Apparent weight in water (\(W_2\)) = \(65\text{ N}\)
    $$\text{Upthrust } (F_B) = W_1 - W_2 = 80 - 65 = \mathbf{15\text{ N}}$$ [2 Marks]

2. Part (ii): Volume of the Solid
    By Archimedes' Principle, Upthrust = Volume of fluid displaced \(\times\) density of fluid \(\times g\).
    Since it is fully submerged, Volume of fluid displaced = Volume of solid (\(V\)).
    $$15 = V \times 1000 \times 10$$
    $$V = \frac{15}{10000} = \mathbf{1.5 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}^3}$$ [2 Marks]

6. Give Scientific Reasons

Q: Why does an iron ship float, but a solid iron nail sinks?
An iron nail is solid, so its overall density is far greater than the density of water; thus, it sinks. A ship, however, is hollow and traps a massive volume of air inside its hull. This dramatically decreases the ship's average density to less than that of water. By Archimedes' Principle, the large volume of the ship displaces a huge weight of water, creating enough upthrust to balance the ship's weight and allow it to float.
Q: Why is it easier to swim in sea water than in river water?
Sea water contains dissolved salts, which makes its density significantly higher than the density of fresh river water. Because upthrust is directly proportional to the density of the fluid (\(F_B = V \rho g\)), sea water exerts a greater upward buoyant force on the swimmer for the same volume displaced. This greater upthrust makes it easier to float and swim.
Q: Why does an ice cube float on water with exactly one-tenth of its volume above the surface?
The density of ice is about \(0.9\text{ g/cm}^3\), while the density of water is \(1.0\text{ g/cm}^3\). The relative density of ice is \(0.9\). According to the Law of Floatation, the fraction of the volume of a floating object that is submerged equals the ratio of its density to the fluid's density. Therefore, \(\frac{9}{10}\) (or 90%) of the ice remains submerged to displace enough water to balance its weight, leaving exactly \(\frac{1}{10}\) (or 10%) exposed above the surface.

8. The Common-Sense Strategy: Reality Checks

Elimination Drill
"Common sense is the law." In science and mathematics, formulas describe physical reality. Top problem solvers don't blindly calculate; they inspect the options first and ask: Which of these are physically absurd or mathematically impossible? Use your physical instincts to eliminate wrong answers before writing down a single equation.
Check 01 Density Ratio Flotation Rule

By the Law of Floatation, the submerged volume fraction strictly equals the ratio of body density to fluid density: \(\frac{V_{\text{sub}}}{V} = \frac{\rho_{\text{body}}}{\rho_{\text{fluid}}}\). Always check this ratio first!

Check 02 Close-Density Implication

When a floating solid's density is very close to that of the liquid (e.g., \(0.92\) vs \(1.025\)), nearly all of the object must be submerged. Options proposing a small submerged fraction (\(<50\%\)) are physically absurd.

Check 03 Percentage Boundary Bracket

For any stable floating body, the submerged fraction must lie strictly within the open interval \(0\% < \frac{V_{\text{sub}}}{V} < 100\%\). A value of \(100\%\) means sinking or neutral suspension, not flotation.

Examiner's Assessment: Correct Apparent Weight Loss
Priya correctly applied Archimedes' Principle: \(\text{Upthrust} = \text{Weight in air} - \text{Weight in water} = 150 - 100 = 50\text{ gf}\). This equals the weight of displaced water. Full credit awarded.
✖ Root Conceptual Blunder Score: 0/1 Mark
Examiner's Assessment: Flawed RD Formula
Fatal formula error! By definition, \(\text{RD} = \frac{\text{Weight of solid in air}}{\text{Loss of weight in water}} = \frac{W_{\text{air}}}{W_{\text{air}} - W_{\text{water}}}\). Priya divided by the apparent weight in water (\(100\text{ gf}\)) instead of the loss of weight (\(50\text{ gf}\)).
⚠ Error Carried Forward (ECF) Score: 1/1 Mark
Examiner's Assessment: Method is Sound (ECF Awarded)
Even though the RD value of \(1.5\) is incorrect, the method \(\rho = \text{RD} \times 1000\text{ kg/m}^3\) is correct. Because Priya carried forward her value with mathematically sound conversion logic, ICSE grants full ECF credit.
Step 1: Calculate apparent loss of weight in water
\[\text{Loss of weight in water} = W_{\text{air}} - W_{\text{water}} = 150\text{ gf} - 100\text{ gf} = 50\text{ gf}\]
Step 2: Calculate Relative Density (RD)
\[\text{RD} = \frac{\text{Weight of body in air}}{\text{Loss of weight in water}} = \frac{150\text{ gf}}{50\text{ gf}} = \mathbf{3.0}\] (Note: Relative density is a pure dimensionless ratio and carries no units.)
Step 3: Calculate Density in SI Units
\[\text{Density} = \text{RD} \times 1000\text{ kg/m}^3 = 3.0 \times 1000 = \mathbf{3000\text{ kg/m}^3}\] (Equivalent to \(3.0\text{ g/cm}^3\) in CGS units.)
