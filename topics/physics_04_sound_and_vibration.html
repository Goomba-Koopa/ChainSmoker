Physics • Topic 04

Sound Waves, Vibration & Acoustics

Production of sound via mechanical vibrations, requirement of a material medium (Bell Jar experiment), speed of sound across states of matter, and characteristics of musical sound (Loudness, Pitch, Quality).

1. Propagation of Sound & Bell Jar Experiment

Requirement of a Material Medium: Sound is a mechanical wave that propagates through longitudinal compressions and rarefactions of matter. Sound cannot travel through a vacuum.

• The Bell Jar Verification: An electric bell is suspended inside an airtight glass bell jar connected to a vacuum pump. When the circuit is closed, the ringing bell is clearly audible. As the vacuum pump evacuates the air inside, the loudness steadily decreases until it becomes completely inaudible, proving sound requires a physical material medium.
Speed of Sound in Different Media at \(20^\circ\text{C}\)
The speed of sound depends directly on the elasticity and density of the medium: $$v_{\text{solids}} > v_{\text{liquids}} > v_{\text{gases}}$$
• Solids (Steel / Iron): \(v \approx 5,000\text{--}5,900\text{ m/s}\)
• Liquids (Pure Water): \(v \approx 1,450\text{--}1,500\text{ m/s}\)
• Gases (Dry Air at STP): \(v \approx 330\text{--}340\text{ m/s}\)

2. Characteristics of Sound: Loudness, Pitch & Timbre

Characteristic Governing Physical Property Sensory Perception & Effect
Loudness Amplitude (\(A\)) of vibration (\(\text{Loudness} \propto A^2\)) Distinguishes a loud sound from a faint sound. Measured in Decibels (\(\text{dB}\)).
Pitch (Shrillness) Frequency (\(f\)) of vibration (\(\text{Pitch} \propto f\)) Distinguishes a shrill (grave) voice (e.g. child, woman) from a deep (flat) voice (e.g. man).
Quality (Timbre) Waveform and harmonic overtones Distinguishes two sounds of the same loudness and pitch played on different instruments.

3. The Fundamental Wave Equation

Mathematical Relation: \(v = f \times \lambda\)
• Let a wave travel a distance equal to its wavelength (\(\lambda\)) in time period (\(T\)).
• Speed \(v = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}} = \frac{\lambda}{T}\).
• Since frequency \(f = \frac{1}{T}\): $$v = f \times \lambda$$ Where \(v\) is speed (\(\text{m/s}\)), \(f\) is frequency (\(\text{Hertz or Hz}\)), and \(\lambda\) is wavelength (\(\text{metres}\)).

• Audible Human Spectrum: \(20\text{ Hz} \le f \le 20,000\text{ Hz}\) (Infrasonic: \(< 20\text{ Hz}\); Ultrasonic: \(> 20,000\text{ Hz}\)).

4. Worked CISCE Exemplar Numerical

Exemplar 4.1: Wave Speed & Echo Timing [3 Marks]
A vibrating tuning fork produces sound waves of frequency \(f = 512\text{ Hz}\) in air. If the wavelength of the sound wave is \(\lambda = 0.65\text{ m}\), calculate: (i) The speed of sound in air, and (ii) The time taken by the sound to travel a distance of \(1,331.2\text{ m}\).
Step-by-Step Examiner Solution:

1. Speed of Sound Calculation:
    $$v = f \times \lambda = 512\text{ Hz} \times 0.65\text{ m} = \mathbf{332.8\text{ m/s}}$$ [1.5 Marks]

2. Time Taken for Propagation:
    $$t = \frac{\text{Distance}}{v} = \frac{1,331.2\text{ m}}{332.8\text{ m/s}} = \mathbf{4.0\text{ seconds}}$$ [1.5 Marks]
