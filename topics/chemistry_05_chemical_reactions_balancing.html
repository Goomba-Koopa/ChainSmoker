Chemical Reactions & Equation Balancing

The Law of Conservation of Mass, the four major reaction categories (Synthesis, Decomposition, Displacement, Double Decomposition), and the Hit-and-Trial balancing masterclass.

1. Law of Conservation of Mass

Fundamental Chemical Axiom: In any chemical reaction, total mass is neither created nor destroyed: $$\sum \text{Mass of Reactants} = \sum \text{Mass of Products}$$
• Why Equations Must Be Balanced: The total number of atoms of each individual element present in the reactants (left-hand side) must exactly equal the total number of atoms of that same element present in the products (right-hand side).

2. The Four Primary Reaction Archetypes

Reaction Type General Formula Canonical Balanced ICSE Chemical Equation
1. Direct Combination (Synthesis) \(\text{A} + \text{B} \longrightarrow \text{AB}\) $$2\text{Mg (s)} + \text{O}_2\text{ (g)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{MgO (s)}$$
2. Decomposition \(\text{AB} \xrightarrow{\Delta / h\nu / \text{elec}} \text{A} + \text{B}\) $$2\text{KClO}_3\text{ (s)} \xrightarrow[\text{MnO}_2]{\Delta} 2\text{KCl (s)} + 3\text{O}_2\uparrow\text{ (g)}$$
3. Single Displacement \(\text{A} + \text{BC} \longrightarrow \text{AC} + \text{B}\) $$\text{Fe (s)} + \text{CuSO}_4\text{ (aq)} \longrightarrow \text{FeSO}_4\text{ (aq)} + \text{Cu (s)}$$
4. Double Decomposition (Precipitation) \(\text{AB} + \text{CD} \longrightarrow \text{AD} + \text{CB}\) $$\text{AgNO}_3\text{ (aq)} + \text{NaCl (aq)} \longrightarrow \text{AgCl}\downarrow\text{ (s, white ppt)} + \text{NaNO}_3\text{ (aq)}$$

3. Hit-and-Trial Balancing Masterclass

Worked Example: Balancing the Combustion of Methane
1. Skeletal Equation: \(\text{CH}_4 + \text{O}_2 \longrightarrow \text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}\)
2. Balance Carbon (C): 1 on LHS, 1 on RHS (Balanced).
3. Balance Hydrogen (H): 4 on LHS (\(\text{CH}_4\)), 2 on RHS (\(\text{H}_2\text{O}\)). Multiply \(\text{H}_2\text{O}\) by 2: $$\text{CH}_4 + \text{O}_2 \longrightarrow \text{CO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$ 4. Balance Oxygen (O): 2 on LHS (\(\text{O}_2\)), \(2 + 2(1) = 4\) on RHS. Multiply \(\text{O}_2\) by 2: $$\mathbf{CH_4\text{ (g)} + 2O_2\text{ (g)} \longrightarrow CO_2\text{ (g)} + 2H_2O\text{ (g)}}$$
