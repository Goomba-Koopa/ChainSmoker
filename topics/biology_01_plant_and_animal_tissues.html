Biology • Topic 01

Plant & Animal Tissue Histology

The cellular organization of multi-cellular organisms, plant tissue classification (Meristematic vs Permanent), vascular transport tissues (Xylem vs Phloem), and the 4 primary animal tissue systems.

1. Meristematic vs. Permanent Plant Tissues

Characteristic Meristematic Tissue Permanent Tissue
Cell Division Capacity Cells are continuously actively dividing by mitosis. Cells have lost the power of division and attained fixed form.
Cell Structure & Wall Small, isodiametric cells with thin cellulose walls. Cells are large, specialized, with thin or thick lignified walls.
Nucleus & Cytoplasm Prominent large nucleus with dense cytoplasm. Small nucleus with large central vacuole.
Intercellular Spaces Completely absent; compact arrangement. Present (e.g. Parenchyma) or absent (e.g. Sclerenchyma).
Locations in Plant Root tips, shoot apices, cambium ring. Leaves, cortex, pith, wood, bark.

2. Vascular Bundles: Xylem vs. Phloem

Comparison Criteria Xylem (Wood) Phloem (Bast)
Conducted Substance Transports water and dissolved mineral salts from soil. Translocates soluble organic food (sucrose) from leaves.
Direction of Transport Strictly unidirectional (upward) from roots to leaves. Bidirectional (multidirectional) to all storage and growing parts.
Living vs Dead Cells Mostly dead elements (Vessels, Tracheids, Fibres; only Parenchyma is living). Mostly living elements (Sieve tubes, Companion cells; only Fibres are dead).
Cell Wall Thickening Thick, heavily lignified walls providing mechanical support. Thin, non-lignified cellulosic cell walls.

3. The 4 Major Animal Tissue Systems

Classification of Animal Tissues: • 1. Epithelial Tissue: Protective surface covering (Squamous, Cuboidal, Columnar, Ciliated).
• 2. Connective Tissue: Binds and supports organs (Areolar, Adipose, Skeletal [Bone, Cartilage], Fluid [Blood, Lymph]).
• 3. Muscular Tissue: Coordinates movement via contractility (Striated / Voluntary, Unstriated / Smooth / Involuntary, Cardiac).
• 4. Nervous Tissue: Coordinates stimuli conduction via elongated Neurons.
