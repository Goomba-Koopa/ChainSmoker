Physics • Topic 09

Magnetism, Field Lines & Electromagnets

Magnetic dipole properties, the proof that repulsion is the only sure test of magnetism, properties of magnetic lines of force, and temporary soft-iron electromagnets.

1. Fundamental Properties of Magnets

Canonical Magnetic Axioms: • Directive Property: A freely suspended bar magnet always aligns itself along the geographic North-South direction.
• Poles in Inseparable Pairs: Monopoles do not exist; breaking a bar magnet produces two smaller complete magnets, each with its own North and South poles.
• Basic Law of Magnetism: Like magnetic poles repel each other; unlike magnetic poles attract each other.

• Why Repulsion is the Only Sure Test of Magnetism: A magnet attracts both an opposite magnetic pole AND any unmagnetized ferromagnetic substance (like soft iron). However, repulsion occurs strictly between two like poles of two distinct magnets. Hence, repulsion alone definitively proves that a given specimen is a magnetized body.

2. Properties of Magnetic Lines of Force

Properties of Magnetic Field Lines
1. Field lines are continuous closed curves emerging from the North Pole and entering the South Pole outside the magnet (and South to North inside the magnet).
2. The relative density (closeness) of field lines indicates the strength of the magnetic field (maximum at the poles).
3. Axiom of Non-Intersection: Two magnetic field lines can never intersect each other. If they did, at the point of intersection there would be two distinct tangents, implying two different directions of magnetic force at a single point in space, which is physically impossible.

3. Electromagnets vs. Permanent Magnets

Comparison Criterion Electromagnet (Soft Iron Core) Permanent Magnet (Steel / Alnico)
Magnetism Nature Temporary; loses magnetism the instant electric current is switched OFF. Permanent; retains magnetic power indefinitely unless heated or hammered.
Core Material Soft Iron (high magnetic permeability, low retentivity). Steel, Alnico (high magnetic retentivity).
Magnetic Strength Control Easily varied by changing the current magnitude or number of coil turns. Fixed strength; cannot be dynamically altered.
Polarity Inversion Reversed simply by reversing the direction of electric current. Fixed polarity; cannot be easily reversed.
ICSE Applications Electric bells, scrapyard lifting cranes, Maglev trains, relays. Magnetic compass needles, loudspeakers, refrigerator door gaskets.
