Biology • Topic 11

Health, Hygiene & Infectious Pathogens

Definition of health (WHO standards), Communicable vs Non-communicable diseases, Microscopic pathogens (Bacteria, Viruses, Fungi, Protozoa), Biological insect vectors (Mosquitoes, Houseflies), and Body immunity mechanisms.

1. Concept of Health & Classification of Diseases

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), health is not merely the absence of disease or infirmity, but a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being. When any organ or organ system fails to function normally, producing specific symptoms and signs, the body enters a state of disease.

Disease Category Transmission Mechanism Primary Causes Everyday Medical Examples
Communicable (Infectious) Diseases Can spread rapidly from an infected person to a healthy person through air, water, food, physical contact, or insect vectors. Living biological pathogens (Bacteria, Viruses, Protozoa, Fungi, Parasitic Worms). • Common Cold & Influenza (Airborne droplets)
• Cholera & Typhoid (Contaminated water/food)
• Malaria & Dengue (Mosquito vector)
Non-Communicable Diseases Remain confined to the individual; cannot spread from one person to another by contact or sharing food. Nutritional deficiencies, genetic defects, organ malfunction, or lifestyle factors. • Scurvy (Vitamin C deficiency)
• Diabetes (Insulin deficiency in pancreas)
• Hypertension & Coronary Heart Disease

2. Major Classes of Microscopic Pathogens

A pathogen is any disease-causing biological organism. The four primary pathogen families infecting humans include:

Pathogen Family Biological Nature Diseases Caused Primary Route of Infection
Bacteria Single-celled microscopic prokaryotes; release toxic metabolic poisons (toxins) inside tissues. • Cholera (Vibrio cholerae)
• Typhoid (Salmonella typhi)
• Tuberculosis (Mycobacterium tuberculosis)		 Contaminated drinking water; airborne sputum droplets from coughs.
Viruses Ultra-microscopic non-cellular genetic particles (DNA/RNA in a protein coat); hijack host cell machinery to multiply.[†1] • Influenza (Flu)
• Polio
• Rabies (transmitted via infected dog/animal bites)
• Dengue Fever		 Sneezing aerosols; blood transmission; mosquito saliva; animal bites.
Protozoa Single-celled microscopic eukaryotic organisms. • Malaria (Plasmodium)
• Amoebic Dysentery (Entamoeba histolytica)		 Bite of female Anopheles mosquito; drinking contaminated pond water.
Fungi Microscopic non-photosynthetic molds and spores. • Ringworm (Tinea)
• Athlete's Foot		 Direct skin-to-skin contact; sharing infected towels, combs, or shoes.
[†1] Medical Nuance: Why Antibiotics Cannot Cure Viral Infections Antibiotics (like Penicillin) work specifically by destroying bacterial cell walls or interfering with bacterial protein synthesis. Because viruses lack a cell wall and reproduce entirely inside human host cells using our own cellular machinery, antibiotics have zero effect on viral illnesses like the common cold, flu, or chickenpox. Taking antibiotics for a viral flu only kills beneficial gut bacteria and breeds dangerous drug-resistant superbugs.

3. Insect Vectors: Biological vs. Mechanical Carriers

A vector is an organism (typically an insect) that carries pathogens from an infected person to a healthy host without suffering from the disease itself.

Insect Vector Type of Carrier Pathogen Transmitted & Disease Preventive Control Measures
Female Anopheles Mosquito Biological Vector: The Plasmodium parasite completes critical sexual stages of its life cycle inside the mosquito's gut and salivary glands. Transmits Malaria into the human bloodstream during blood meals (females need protein to lay eggs; males only sip nectar). Eliminate standing puddles; introduce Gambusia fish to eat mosquito larvae; use mosquito bed nets.
Female Aedes Mosquito Biological Vector: Active daytime biter (tiger mosquito with white striped legs). Transmits Dengue virus and Chikungunya virus. Empty stagnant water from flowerpots, discarded tires, and open roof tanks.
Common Housefly (Musca domestica) Mechanical Vector: Carries pathogens on hairy legs and sticky mouthparts after landing on sewage, then vomits digestive juices onto uncovered human food. Transmits Cholera, Typhoid, and Amoebiasis. Keep food strictly covered under wire mesh; maintain clean kitchen sanitation; dispose of household garbage in sealed bins.

4. Worked Logical Diagnostic Exemplars

Problem 11.1: Epidemiological Outbreak Investigation
During the monsoon season, fifty residents of a village suddenly develop high fever accompanied by violent shivering (chills) every 48 hours, followed by profuse sweating. A swamp near the village has extensive stagnant water.
1. Diagnose the disease.
2. Name the causative pathogen and its transmitting vector.
3. Explain the biological logic behind spraying kerosene oil on the stagnant swamp surface.
Step-by-Step Epidemiological Diagnosis:

1. Diagnosis: The recurring cycle of violent shivering chills, high fever, and profuse sweating every 48 hours is the classic clinical symptom of Malaria.

2. Pathogen & Vector:
• Causative Pathogen: The protozoan parasite Plasmodium vivax.
• Transmitting Vector: The Female Anopheles mosquito.

3. Logic of Kerosene Spraying:
• Mosquito larvae and pupae breathe atmospheric air by protruding a tiny breathing tube (respiratory siphon) through the water surface tension film.
• Spraying a thin film of kerosene oil drastically reduces surface tension and physically blocks the siphon tubes, cutting off oxygen and suffocating the larvae before they can mature into adult biting mosquitoes.
Problem 11.2: Vaccination & Active Immunity Mechanism
A healthy 6-month-old infant receives an injection of the oral polio vaccine. Explain: (i) What a vaccine actually contains, and (ii) The logical biological chain of events through which the vaccine provides lifelong protection without causing illness.
Step-by-Step Immunological Resolution:

1. Composition of a Vaccine: A vaccine contains dead, weakened (attenuated), or fragmented parts of the pathogen that retain their surface recognition flags (antigens) but are incapable of producing full-blown disease.

2. Biological Defense Chain:
• Recognition: The infant's white blood cells (lymphocytes) detect the foreign viral antigens in the vaccine.
• Antibody Production: Lymphocytes produce specialized protective protein molecules called antibodies tailored to neutralize that specific pathogen.
• Immunological Memory: Long-lived memory cells are formed. If the child is exposed to live, wild poliovirus years later, the memory cells immediately recognize the virus and mass-produce lethal antibodies instantly, destroying the virus before it can establish an infection.

5. Give Scientific Reasons

Immunology 1: Vaccination & Immunity
How does administering a vaccine protect a child from future infectious bacterial and viral diseases?
Scientific Resolution:
A vaccine introduces dead or weakened (attenuated) antigens into the bloodstream. This stimulates white blood cells (B-lymphocytes) to produce specific antibodies and generate long-lasting memory cells without causing actual disease. Upon future real infection, memory cells rapidly produce massive antibody levels.
Immunology 2: Antibiotics Ineffective Against Viruses
Why are antibiotics like penicillin completely ineffective in treating the common cold or viral influenza?
Scientific Resolution:
Antibiotics specifically target and destroy bacterial cell walls and bacterial metabolic pathways. Viruses are acellular particles lacking cell walls and metabolic machinery, relying entirely on host cells for replication, making them immune to antibiotic action.
