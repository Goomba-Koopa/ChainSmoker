Translatory Motion & The Rigid Body

When a body undergoes translatory motion, every point inside it moves through the same distance along parallel lines in equal intervals of time. If you slide a flat notebook across a smooth wooden desk, the vector connecting two marked points on the front edge remains strictly parallel to its new position ($AB \parallel A'B'$).

Key Distinction: Rectilinear translation travels along a straight line; curvilinear translation follows a curve, but the orientation of the line segments never rotates.

Deriving average velocity from first principles: $v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t}$. In SI units, measured strictly in meters per second ($\text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$).
