Physics • Hydrostatic Fluid Mechanics Worked Problem
A solid metal block of mass \(60\text{ kg}\) measures \(0.5\text{ m} \times 0.4\text{ m} \times 0.2\text{ m}\). Calculate (i) the maximum pressure, and (ii) the minimum pressure it can exert on a horizontal table. [Take \(g = 10\text{ m/s}^2\)]
Step-by-Step Logical Resolution:

1. Applied Thrust (Downward Force):
    The gravitational thrust is the full weight of the metal block:
    $$F = m \cdot g = 60\text{ kg} \times 10\text{ m/s}^2 = \mathbf{600\text{ N}}$$

2. Maximum Pressure (Concentrated on Smallest Base Area):
    Pressure is inversely proportional to contact area (\(P = F/A\)). The smallest face is \(A_{\text{min}} = 0.4\text{ m} \times 0.2\text{ m} = 0.08\text{ m}^2\):
    $$P_{\text{max}} = \frac{600\text{ N}}{0.08\text{ m}^2} = \mathbf{7,500\text{ Pa (or N/m}^2\mathbf{)}}$$

3. Minimum Pressure (Spread over Largest Base Area):
    The largest face is \(A_{\text{max}} = 0.5\text{ m} \times 0.4\text{ m} = 0.20\text{ m}^2\):
    $$P_{\text{min}} = \frac{600\text{ N}}{0.20\text{ m}^2} = \mathbf{3,000\text{ Pa (or N/m}^2\mathbf{)}}$$
Physical Intuition: Standing the block on its smallest end concentrates the 600 N weight onto a tiny surface, yielding 2.5× higher pressure than resting it flat.
